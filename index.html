ASC specks and α-synuclein amplify microglial NLRP3 inflammasome activation and co-localize with Lewy body pathology🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-10 · Thakur, K., Peladeau, C., Hewitt, M. … Tomlinson, J. T., Schlossmacher, M. G., Sandhu, J. K.（共 9 位作者）
关键词『Parkinson、amyloid』(命中abstract)
Neuroinflammation is a hallmark of several chronic neurodegenerative disorders, including Parkinson's disease (PD) and other synucleinopathies, and is closely associated with microglial activation. The inflammasome adaptor protein apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a caspase activation and recruitment domain (ASC) undergoes oligomerization upon inflammasome activation, resulting in the formation of ASC specks. These structures are recognized by microglia as damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), thereby promoting inflammatory signaling. Recent studies have demonstrated that extracellular ASC specks associate with amyloid-{beta} (A{beta}), and that uptake of ASC-A{beta} composites by microglia amplifies pro-inflammatory responses. Although ASC specks have also been shown to potentiate -synuclein (Syn) preformed fibril-induced pathology, their interaction with monomeric Syn and their effects on inflammasome activation remain poorly understood. Here, we co-stimulated murine SIM-A9 microglia with ASC-Syn assemblies and evaluated their effects on NLRP3 inflammasome activation. Electron microscopy revealed a close association between ASC fibrils and Syn monomers. Exposure of microglia to ASC-Syn enhanced NLRP3 inflammasome activation, as evidenced by increased ASC speck formation, caspase-1 activation, and secretion of IL-1{beta} and IL-18, when compared to ASC or Syn alone. In addition, immunohistochemical analyses of post mortem brain tissue from patients with dementia with Lewy bodies demonstrated ASC expression in regions exhibiting Syn pathology and showed ASC co-localization with Syn within Lewy bodies. Collectively, these findings identify ASC-Syn assemblies as potent amplifiers of NLRP3 inflammasome signaling and suggest a mechanism through which they may contribute to sustained neuroinflammation in synucleinopathies.

A Bayesian approach to volumetric analysis in rare neurodegenerative disorders🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-10 · Ho, S. Y. S., Schmidt, T. V., Ghosh, B., Rowe, J., Rittman, T.
关键词『Parkinson』(命中abstract)
Identifying regional brain atrophy is critical for understanding neurodegenerative disorders. Traditionally, group comparisons rely on frequentist statistics, utilising $p$-values and point estimates that offer limited characterization of uncertainty. Bayesian methods provide an alternative by estimating effect sizes and credible intervals while incorporating prior knowledge and uncertainty. This study evaluated the feasibility of Bayesian hierarchical modelling to assess regional brain atrophy in Progressive Supranuclear Palsy (PSP) and Corticobasal Syndrome (CBS) compared to healthy controls. Participants meeting diagnostic criteria for CBS or PSP, together with healthy controls, were recruited from the Cambridge Centre for Parkinson-plus. Grey matter volume, cortical thickness, and subcortical volumes were derived using FreeSurfer for 210 cortical and 36 subcortical regions defined by the Human Brainnetome Atlas. Participants with PSP were classified into Richardson syndrome (PSP-RS), corticobasal syndrome variant (PSP-CBS), frontal variant (PSP-F), and other presentations (PSP-Other). Bayesian hierarchical ANOVA models with appropriate priors were used to estimate group differences. Regions of practical equivalence (ROPE) were calculated to assess evidence of meaningful group effects. A total of 428 people were included (113 PSP-RS, 30 PSP-CBS, 23 PSP-Other, 19 PSP-F, 81 CBS, and 162 healthy controls). For grey matter volume, there was evidence of whole brain volume loss compared to controls for all disease groups except PSP-Other, which showed evidence of no difference. For cortical thickness, there was evidence of global cortical thinning in CBS compared to controls, but for no other group. For subcortical volume all groups showed evidence of volume loss compared to controls. Patterns of regional atrophy were identified in keeping with previous literature, with the largest effect observed in the left pre-motor thalamus (30\% reduction in CBS). There was greater loss of subcortical volume in PSP-RS compared to other PSP subgroups. Bayesian hierarchical modelling provides a feasible framework for quantifying regional brain atrophy, with interpretable effect size estimates and uncertainty measures. This approach complements conventional statistical methods, particularly in the assessment of rare neurodegenerative and neuropsychiatric disorders.

Encephalitic alphavirus infection reduces neurite outgrowth and downregulates Rho GTPase pathways in human iPSC-derived neurospheres🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-09 · Cirksena, K., Haiber, L. M., Kuehne, B. A. … Konzok, S., Seeger, B., Gerold, G.（共 15 位作者）
关键词『amyloid』(命中abstract)
Venezuelan equine encephalitis virus (VEEV) is a re-emerging neurotropic arbovirus, which affects the developing human brain, potentially leading to miscarriage, stillbirth, or long-term neurological sequelae. Currently, the mechanisms underlying these sequelae remain largely elusive. Using human induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived neurospheres as a model for brain development, we show that both human neurons and glial cells are susceptible to VEEV infection. Microscopy-based quantification reveals that VEEV impairs neurite outgrowth in the human neurospheres. To mechanistically assess these observations, we performed multi-omics analyses of VEEV-infected neurospheres and demonstrate translational shutoff including downregulation of Rho GTPase pathways. The transcriptional and proteome profile is indicative of reduced actin cytoskeleton dynamics and consequently migratory defects of cells in the neurospheres. Shotgun secretomics moreover revealed downmodulation of neurite outgrowth associated proteins including midkine (MDK), amyloid-beta precursor protein (APP) and microtubule-associated protein 1B (MAP1B). Our findings reveal mechanistic insights into how VEEV infection impairs neuronal development and demonstrate that neurospheres are a powerful platform for assessing teratogenic effects of viruses on brain development.
