Sex differences in astrocytic reactivity and its association with cognitive decline in cognitively unimpaired older adults🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-10 · Reichert Plaska, C., Jacobs, T., Bruno, D. … Zetterberg, H., Pomara, N., Alzheimers Disease Neuroimaging Initiative,（共 11 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer、tau』(命中abstract)
INTRODUCTION: Astrocyte reactivity may contribute to increased Alzheimers disease (AD) risk in women. Although plasma glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) levels have been reported to be higher in women than in men, few studies have examined sex-specific associations with other plasma AD biomarkers and cognition. METHODS: Participants were enrolled in the Memory Education and Research Initiative, a longitudinal community-based cohort, and underwent evaluation including blood biomarker sampling. The Alzheimers Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) was included as a replication cohort. RESULTS: Females had higher plasma GFAP levels than males in MERI (N{equiv}584), a finding that was replicated in ADNI. In MERI, higher baseline GFAP was associated with lower A{beta}42/40 and higher p-Tau231 and NfL levels. Higher baseline GFAP was also associated with steeper longitudinal decline in global cognition and episodic memory; however, GFAP x sex x time interactions were not significant. In ADNI, higher baseline GFAP was associated with higher p-Tau217 and NfL levels and with steeper longitudinal decline in global cognition and selected executive and semantic measures, without consistent evidence of sex-specific longitudinal effects. DISCUSSION: Plasma GFAP levels were consistently higher in females across two independent cohorts. GFAP was associated with AD-related blood biomarkers and longitudinal cognitive decline, but sex did not consistently modify these associations. These findings support a role for astrocytic reactivity in early AD-related processes while underscoring the need for further replication of sex-specific effects.

Subcellular Spatial Profiling Unveils Links Between Neuritic Plaques with Selective Tau Vulnerability in Alzheimer's Disease🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-10 · Li, H., Gross, E. R., Devine, A. … Zwang, T. J., Bennett, R. E., Das, S.（共 14 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer、tau、amyloid』(命中title/abstract)
Alzheimer's disease (AD) features widespread extracellular amyloid-{beta} plaques but selective intraneuronal tau accumulation in cortical layers II/III and V, while layer IV remains relatively resistant. Here, we combined subcellular spatial transcriptomics with co-registered A{beta} and pTau immunohistochemistry to determine why neurons exposed to comparable plaque-associated pathology differ in somatic pTau accumulation. Plaque-associated neuritic stress occurred similarly across layers, but layer IV neurons showed attenuated pTau accumulation and reduced somatic expression of a shared neurite-soma stress program involving DCTN1, CALM3, NORAD, and PIN1. Trajectory analysis within layers identified tau-vulnerable and resistant neuronal states implicating calcium signaling, retrograde transport, and lysosomal function. Notably, astrocytic APOE-neuronal LRP1 co-expression increased significantly along the pTau-vulnerable trajectory, and APOE {epsilon}4 was associated with stronger coupling of local plaque pathology to pTau-associated molecular programs. These findings reveal neuronal programs and neuron-glia interactions underlying selective tau vulnerability and resistance in AD, highlighting potential tau-targeting therapeutics.

Beyond organ age gaps: comparing plasma proteomic metrics of organ biology in relation to brain structure and neurodegenerative disease.🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-10 · Zendehdel, R., An, L., Chauveau, L. … Bethlehem, R. A. I., Pichet-Binette, A., Vogel, J.（共 11 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer』(命中abstract)
Biological aging is heterogeneous across organ systems, and brain health may be influenced by biological processes extending beyond the central nervous system (CNS). This can be investigated using biological age measures that are increasingly used to estimate how biological systems deviate from chronological aging. However, organ level plasma proteomic metrics may capture different forms of biological variation depending on how they are defined. Here, we compared three complementary organ level proteomic metrics across 15 organ systems (and two global systems) in relation to age, organ-relevant physiological phenotypes, brain morphometry, and incident neurodegenerative disease, using plasma proteomics from 52,667 UK Biobank participants and brain MRI data from 5,078 of those individuals. We derived i) supervised age gap metrics using XGBoost models trained to predict chronological age, ii) unsupervised PC1 metrics capturing dominant organ-associated proteomic variation, and iii) phenotype guided PLS metrics linking plasma proteins to organ-relevant physiological traits. The three metric classes were related but not interchangeable. Global all-protein and all-organ-protein models showed strong age-related proteomic structure. However, organ-specific PC analyses showed that age was often but not always the dominant source of variation in protein signal across organs. Age gap metrics showed the most consistent associations with subsequent ICD-10-based neurodegenerative and vascular diagnoses, whereas all three metric classes showed distinct associations with brain structure and organ-relevant physiological phenotypes. Brain structure analyses revealed heterogeneous organ-brain association patterns across organs, suggesting that proteomic variation relates to brain structure in both a systemic and an organ-specific manner. Several systemic and organ-specific signals partially mediated the associations between disease specific genetic risk and incident diagnoses of Alzheimer's disease and stroke, albeit with small effect sizes (1-8%). Similarly, organ metrics provided modest incremental predictive improvement over baseline demographic models, with the largest increase in ROC-AUC observed for Alzheimer's disease when all organ age gap metrics were added jointly (delta ROC-AUC = 0.037). These results link brain related outcomes to proteomic variation across both CNS and non-CNS systems. Our work suggests that proteomic organ metrics capture complementary dimensions of biological variation rather than a single construct of organ aging. Although these measures may provide informative biological and potentially predictive signals, their incremental predictive value in the present analyses was modest.

Pharmaco-genetic evidence of presymptomatic lysosomal dysfunction coupled to detrimental DPR secretion in Drosophila Models of C9orf72-linked ALS/FTD.🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-10 · Smeele, P. H., Ferrari, V., Spina, G. … Cauchi, R., Poletti, A., Vaccari, T.（共 7 位作者）
关键词『amyotrophic lateral sclerosis、neurodegeneration』(命中abstract)
Hexanucleotide repeat expansions in C9orf72, the most common genetic cause of Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and frontotemporal dementia (FTD), drive neurodegeneration through both loss of gene function and toxic gain-of-function mechanisms, including transcription of repeat RNA and repeat-associated non-AUG (RAN) translation into dipeptide repeat proteins (DPRs). Both disease mechanisms have been linked to disruption of the autophagy-lysosome pathway (ALP), though how this contributes to disease progression remains unclear. By expressing toxic C9orf72 repeats in Drosophila melanogaster, we observed presymptomatic lysosome impairment, coupled with largely preserved autophagosome formation. Pharmacological treatment with valproic acid (VPA) partially prevented neurodegenerative and locomotor phenotypes in expressing animals in presence of Mitf, which is required for lysosomal biogenesis. Genetic inhibition of residual lysosomal degradation enhanced neurotoxicity, whereas downregulation of autophagy and secretory autophagy genes suppressed toxicity, uncovering a previously unrecognized contribution of secretion to disease. Consistent with this, in vitro experiments indicated that disabling lysosome function, but not autophagy induction, leads to an increase in neuronal release of autophagy-associated proteins and DPRs, which can be found be uptaken by nearby neurons and glia in vivo. We propose that repeat-driven presymptomatic lysosomal dysfunction, prevented by pharmacologic treatment, promotes secretory autophagy, which, in turn facilitates extracellular release and intercellular propagation of neurotoxic DPRs.

Spatial proteomics on repurposed sequencers reveals early astrocytic remodeling in an ALS mouse model🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-10 · Pandit, K., Fouts, C., Rodwin, S. … Phatnani, H., Pleska, M., Vickovic, S.（共 12 位作者）
关键词『amyotrophic lateral sclerosis』(命中abstract)
Spatial proteomics can reveal how disease reshapes cells and their local environments, but extending these measurements across replicated tissue cohorts requires validated antibodies, consistent sample processing and integrated analysis. These requirements are particularly pressing in studies of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), where pathology evolves unevenly across spinal-cord regions and disease stages. Here, we established an end-to-end spatial proteomics workflow linking a validated 28-antibody panel to automated fluorescence decoding and imaging on repurposed high-throughput sequencers, followed by multicycle image processing, cellular profiling and probabilistic spatial-neighborhood analysis. We applied it to 70 lumbar spinal-cord sections from nine SOD1-G93A and nine wild-type mice at presymptomatic, disease-onset and symptomatic stages. Spatial neighborhood analysis revealed that the environments surrounding motor neurons were already remodeled at disease onset. GFAP-high neighborhoods expanded in the ventral gray matter, with 43% of motor neurons embedded in these environments in SOD1-G93A mice, compared with 15% in wild-type mice. This early expansion preceded the later decline of non-reactive gray matter and detectable motor-neuron loss. The regional organization of the protein-defined changes shared features with neuronal and astrocytic patterns mapped by spatial transcriptomics in mouse and human ALS. This work enables spatial proteomics across replicated tissue cohorts and identifies localized remodeling of the environments around surviving motor neurons as an early feature of disease in the SOD1-G93A model.

Novel Tau Interaction Partners as Potential Biomarkers and Therapeutic Targets in Neurodegenerative Diseases🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-09 · Pichlerova, K., Sinsky, J., Njemoga, S. … Cente, M., Matyasova, K., Hanes, J.（共 10 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer、tau』(命中abstract/title)
Alzheimers disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder leading to cognitive decline and ultimately death. One of the main hallmarks of this disease is the accumulation of pathological tau protein into neurofibrillary tangles. To better understand these pathological mechanisms, it is essential to characterize the tau protein interactome in health and disease. In this study, we performed a yeast two-hybrid screening using a human brain cDNA library, from which we identified 15 novel tau-interacting proteins that bind to full-length human tau, or its fragments: the N-terminus, microtubule-binding domain, or C-terminus. We subsequently validated these novel interactions using in vitro co-immunoprecipitation and colocalization assays with both physiological and pathological tau isoforms. Furthermore, their interactions with the human tau protein were analyzed using surface plasmon resonance. Finally, an in silico interactome analysis of these newly discovered tau-binding partners revealed several additional interactors that can link them to downstream processes critical to neurodegenerative pathways Our findings offer fresh insights into tau biology and highlight novel targets for therapeutic intervention and biomarker development.

Ogg1 regulates context-dependent glial states in Alzheimer's disease🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-09 · Abentung, A., Sakshaug, T., Schnur, P. … Kunath, N., Bjoras, M., Scheffler, K.（共 11 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer、amyloid』(命中title/abstract)
Oxidative DNA damage is an early hallmark of Alzheimer's disease (AD), but whether Ogg1, a key enzyme in oxidative DNA repair, contributes to disease pathogenesis remains unclear. Here, we show that Ogg1 exerts opposing effects under physiological and disease conditions. Ogg1 deficiency impaired memory and induced glial transcriptional alterations under physiological conditions, whereas in APP/PS1 mice it improved memory, reduced amyloid burden and attenuated AD-associated glial remodeling. These effects occurred without changes in global 8-oxo-dG levels and were accompanied by changes in chromatin accessibility and gene expression, supporting a non-canonical role for Ogg1 in transcriptional regulation. Single-nucleus transcriptomics revealed strong transcriptional responses to Ogg1 deficiency across all major glial cell types and showed that Ogg1-dependent glial transcriptional programs are strongly conserved in human AD. These findings identify Ogg1 as a context-dependent regulator of glial cell states and reveal an unexpected role in AD-associated glial remodeling.

First-in-class -PET radiotracers for GPR39: A platform for noninvasive target engagement imaging in vivo🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-09 · Bhoopal, B., Fitzgerald, R. W., Gollapelli, K. … Jadiya, P., Cerevera-Juanes, R., Solingapuram Sai, K. K.（共 12 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer、neurodegeneration』(命中abstract)
Disrupted zinc homeostasis is a hallmark of Alzheimer's disease. As a key zinc-sensing receptor, GPR39, a G protein-coupled receptor implicated in neurodegeneration, vascular regulation, and neuropsychiatric disorders, represents an emerging target for molecular imaging. However, the lack of suitable positron emission tomography (PET) radiotracers has limited the in vivo characterization of GPR39. Quantitative assessment of GPR39 in vivo could improve our understanding of its role in physiology and disease and facilitate the development and evaluation of targeted therapeutics. Here, we introduce [18F]GPR39 2.1 as a PET radiotracer for specific imaging of GPR39. [18F]GPR39 2.1 exhibited excellent serum stability with negligible radiometabolite formation. In vitro cell uptake studies demonstrated a 55-70% reduction in tracer uptake following pharmacological blockade. PET imaging showed rapid brain uptake, efficient blood-brain barrier penetration, and gradual washout. Blockade studies in rats demonstrated significant reductions in brain uptake of up to approximately 90% (p<0.0001) across multiple brain regions. GPR39 specificity was further validated in GPR39 knockout mice, which showed 76% (p<0.002) and 90% (p<0.0002) reductions in brain uptake by PET imaging and biodistribution, respectively. Moreover, 5xFAD transgenic mice exhibited significantly lower brain uptake by PET imaging (40%, p<0.0002) and biodistribution (77%, p<0.002), indicating sensitivity of [18F]GPR39 2.1 to disease-associated alterations in GPR39. Biodistribution studies demonstrated favorable pharmacokinetics, while autoradiography further confirmed GPR39-specific binding, consistent with the PET and biodistribution findings. Collectively, these results establish [18F]GPR39 2.1 as a stable, brain-penetrant, and highly specific PET radiotracer for noninvasive imaging of GPR39 in vivo. This work provides a translational framework for investigating GPR39 biology and supports the development of GPR39 as an imaging and therapeutic target in neurological and systemic diseases associated with GPR39 dysregulation.

Preemptive SOD1 Silencing via Neonatal Intramuscular AAV Therapy Modifies Disease Trajectory in an ALS Mouse Model🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-09 · Gong, X., Xie, Y., Wei, H. … Bian, Z., Wang, W., Xu, T.（共 8 位作者）
关键词『amyotrophic lateral sclerosis』(命中abstract)
Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) is a progressive and fatal neurodegenerative disorder with limited therapeutic options. A major challenge for developing effective therapies is the safe and efficient delivery of genetic cargo to the spinal motor neurons that degenerate in the disease. This is particularly pertinent for SOD1-linked familial ALS, where gene silencing approaches are promising but remain constrained by suboptimal delivery. Here, we establish a gene delivery platform that overcomes this barrier by combining the retrograde transport of rAAV2-retro with an artificial miRNA to achieve pan-spinal SOD1 silencing. A single intramuscular injection of rAAV2-retro-miRNA into neonatal SOD1G93A mice achieved widespread transduction of spinal motor neurons, significant reduction of mutant SOD1 protein, and conferred multifaceted therapeutic benefits. Treated mice exhibited delayed disease onset, extended lifespan, preserved motor function, reduced neuroinflammation, and protection of neuromuscular junctions and spinal motor neurons. These findings establish the rAAV2-retro-miRNA platform as a versatile and efficient tool for spinal motor neuron-targeted gene silencing, addressing the delivery challenge and providing a foundation for further development in motor neuron diseases.
