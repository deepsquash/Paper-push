Linking Molecular Changes in the Hypothalamus to Alzheimer's Disease Phenotypes🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-25 · Algoo, J., Kaczorowski, C. C., Dunn, A. R., Menon, V., O'Connell, K. M. S.
关键词『Alzheimer、amyloid』(命中title/abstract)
In many individuals with Alzheimer's disease (AD), sleep disruption and weight loss emerge years before the onset of memory decline and dementia. However, the cellular and molecular bases of these early events are not well understood. The hypothalamus is a critical brain region for the regulation of these behaviors, but its response to amyloid has not been systematically characterized. We performed single-nucleus RNA sequencing to generate transcriptomic profiles of 1.1 million nuclei across the hypothalamus from 10 genetic backgrounds of the AD-BXD genetic reference panel spanning two ages and both sexes, including age-matched controls. Amyloid was associated with widespread, sex-biased molecular changes across diverse cell classes, including elevated neuroinflammatory signatures in microglia and astrocytes, upregulated ion transport pathways across barrier-associated cell types, and sex- and age-dependent changes to primary ciliary pathways in Glp1r+/Lepr+, Histaminergic, and Hcrt+ neuronal populations. Two metabolism-related populations showed differential expression exclusively in one sex: Glp1r+/Lepr+ neurons in females and Gpr50+ neurons in males. Several findings were conserved in analysis of human hypothalamus snRNAseq data, including reduced expression of TGF-{beta} pathway genes in medial mammillary body neurons and increased relative abundance of reactive Serpina3n+/YKL-40 astrocytes. This atlas provides a cellular framework for understanding the early, non-cognitive symptoms of AD and identifies several hypothalamic cell-type specific pathways that represent potential targets for therapeutic intervention.

An Open, Evolving Brain and Cognitive Aging Simulator Enabled by Agentic Artificial Intelligence🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-25 · Buckner, R. L., Walhovd, K. B., Fjell, A. M. … Rogalski, E. J., Hyman, B., Nyberg, L.（共 8 位作者）
关键词『amyloid』(命中abstract)
An agentic AI simulator was built to advance our understanding about how the human brain and cognition change with age. The simulator generates worlds based on hypothesized age-linked cascades and then simulates studies across varied experimental designs and selection criteria. Each simulated world begins with 120,000 individual lives initiated by baseline parameters (e.g., genetic risk, birth year) and then changed by age-dependent cascades (e.g., amyloid-dependent, cardiovascular). Examples illustrate how the simulator predicts distinct data patterns in cross-sectional versus longitudinal designs, how alternative participant selection strategies emphasize baseline differences versus age-related decline, and how variable levels of association emerge between brain and cognition dependent upon design choices. Simulations of clinical trials provide information about design, power, and outcome considerations. Experiments on alternative simulated worlds evaluate how well hypothesized cascades accommodate real-world data patterns, capturing the insights and open puzzles inside the application. Most critically, the application is built to evolve. Its code contains a complete description of how to construct and update itself so that any person or AI agent can explore a new finding, recalibrate to emerging data, or build and simulate a novel future study. The simulator is offered as a tool for understanding data patterns, teaching, and generation of hypotheses to be tested in future studies using real-world data collection.

Neural mechanisms by which spaced learning promotes understanding🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-25 · Liang, Y., He, Y., Cheng, Z. … Luo, W., Yang, X., Zhang, L.（共 12 位作者）
关键词『tau』(命中abstract)
Spaced learning reliably improves memory. A few studies suggest it also benefits understanding and problem solving, but none has examined the neural mechanism. We hypothesised that this benefit arises because spacing changes the memory trace rather than merely strengthening it. To test this, we taught participants how unfamiliar words meanings could be derived from familiar parts: meaningful rather than rote learning. Each participant learned words in one session, or in two sessions about 2 h (massed) or 48 h (spaced) apart, to the same criterion; one-session words served as the reference. A common interval separated the last session from a delayed test with electroencephalography (EEG). Immediately after learning, self-rated understanding did not differ between schedules, yet under spacing participants revised more of their written test responses between sessions (35% versus 25%). At the delayed test, the spacing effect appeared: recognition was higher and self-rated explanatory retrieval more fluent after spaced than after massed learning. At the neural level, late-retrieval EEG patterns for remembered words were less similar to the reference after spaced than after massed learning, over right frontocentral and temporal sensors. In partly overlapping sensors and windows, lower similarity was statistically associated with a larger fluency advantage, suggesting that the change is directed rather than random. Behavioural evidence hinted at convergence across learners: between sessions, revisions under spacing were smaller and moved responses toward those of others. Together, these findings suggest that spacing changes the memory trace rather than merely strengthening it, in a direction linked to self-rated understanding. Significance StatementSpacing out study helps memory, but any benefit for understanding may not show when study ends. Each learner studied how familiar parts explained unfamiliar words meanings, revisiting some after about two hours and others after about two days. Both schedules ended at the same mastery criterion, with similar ratings of understanding. Yet with the longer gap, learners had revised written explanations more often, in smaller steps toward other learners wording. After an equal delay, they rated those explanations easier to reconstruct. Brain activity during retrieval was consistent with a memory that spacing had reorganised, not merely strengthened, in a way linked to those ratings. The moment a learner feels they understand may be too early to judge a study schedule.

Laterality of subcortical structures predicts spontaneous brain dynamics🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-24 · Ghafari, T., Quinn, A. J., Jensen, O.
关键词『neurodegeneration』(命中abstract)
Subcortical structures contribute to neocortical activity through extensive circuits supporting cognition and behaviour. Although these anatomical connections are well established, how subcortical structure relates to neocortical oscillations remains poorly understood. Previous studies have linked subcortical volume asymmetries to cortical alpha modulation during attention tasks, but whether these relationships extend to spontaneous brain activity remains unclear. Here, we investigated whether hemispheric asymmetries in subcortical volume are associated with lateralised resting-state oscillatory power. We analysed structural MRI and resting-state magnetoencephalography (MEG) data from 532 healthy adults in the Cambridge Centre for Ageing and Neuroscience (CamCAN) cohort, correlating volume asymmetries of seven subcortical structures with MEG power asymmetries across four frequency bands. We found that globus pallidus asymmetry was positively associated with alpha-band power lateralisation, extending previous task-based findings to the resting state. Within the striatum, putamen asymmetry was positively associated with frontal beta-band lateralisation, whereas caudate asymmetry was negatively associated with posterior beta- and delta-band lateralisation. Hippocampal asymmetry was negatively associated with frontal delta-band lateralisation. Despite the complexity of cortico-subcortical connectivity, these findings reveal distinct frequency-specific relationships between subcortical anatomy and spontaneous neocortical activity that align with the established functional roles of these structures. Our results demonstrate that these relationships can be detected non-invasively in resting state without requiring direct recording of deep brain regions, providing a foundation for investigating whether resting-state MEG can identify early functional changes associated with subcortical neurodegeneration.
