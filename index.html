Astrocyte-Specific dsRNA-ADAR1 Signalling Links Innate Immunity to Complement Activation in Parkinson Disease🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-12 · Liu, Y., Halliday, G., Fu, Y.
关键词『Parkinson、alpha-synuclein』(命中title/abstract)
Background: Dysregulated innate immune activation is a key feature of Parkinson disease (PD), but the upstream triggers and cell-type-specific mechanisms remain unclear. Double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) has emerged as a potential driver of innate immune responses. Adenosine deaminase acting on RNA 1 (ADAR1), particularly its interferon-inducible p150 isoform, edits dsRNA to limit aberrant immune activation. Whether this dsRNA-ADAR1 axis contributes to neuroinflammation in PD in a cell-type-specific manner is unknown. Methods: We performed immunofluorescence on post-mortem cingulate cortex tissue from PD cases and controls to localise dsRNA, ADAR1 p150 and pathological alpha-synuclein protein. We integrated these findings with single-nucleus RNA sequencing data from three independent human brain datasets. An eight-module gene scoring framework was applied to assess dsRNA processing, sensing, and downstream innate immune pathways in neurons and astrocytes. Results: In PD cortex, the proportion of dsRNA-positive astrocytes was higher than in controls, whereas neuronal dsRNA positivity did not differ between groups. ADAR1 p150 positivity increased in both astrocytes and neurons, and the proportion of ADAR1 p150 positive astrocytes correlated with astrocytic -synuclein burden. Transcriptomic analyses showed that neurons were enriched for dsRNA editing and processing machinery, whereas astrocytes exhibited higher expression of dsRNA sensing pathways, innate immune responses, and transposable element control. While overall pathway activity did not differ between PD and controls after covariate adjustment, network analyses revealed cell-type-specific coupling patterns. Notably, complement gene expression showed a strong and selective association with dsRNA-related pathways in astrocytes, whereas interferon and NF-{kappa}B signalling were shared across cell types. In both cell types, ADAR1-associated gene programmes were enriched for oxidative phosphorylation and PD-related pathways. Conclusions: These findings identify astrocytes as a central site of relative dsRNA accumulation and ADAR1 p150 induction in the PD cortex. The selective coupling of dsRNA sensing to complement activation in astrocytes highlights a cell-type-specific innate immune pathway that is constitutively present rather than induced by disease. Together, our protein-level and network-level findings converge on a coherent and testable model in which a constitutive astrocytic circuit is engaged by disease-associated dsRNA accumulation, with potential relevance for disease progression and therapeutic targeting.

Single Cell Proteomics reveals partial uncoupling of the Glycerol-3-Phosphate shuttle to compensate for Electron Transport Chain dysfunction in Alexander Disease GFAP mutations🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-12 · van Gelder, C. A., Daoutsali, E., Attour, Y. … Vos, H. R., Hol, E. M., Burgering, B. M.（共 11 位作者）
关键词『neurodegeneration』(命中abstract)
Alexander disease (AxD) is a rare genetic leukodystrophy caused by dominant mutations in the gene coding for the astrocytic intermediate filament protein Glial Fibrillary Acidic Protein (GFAP). Understanding disease mechanisms is complicated by the fact that mutant GFAP promotes further GFAP accumulation, making it difficult to distinguish mutation-specific effects from those caused by elevated GFAP levels. Here, we performed mass spectrometry-based single cell proteomics to investigate proteomic alterations associated with increasing levels of wildtype GFAP and a common AxD-causing mutant GFAP R239C. By analyzing individual cells spanning a broad range of GFAP expression levels, we separated dosage-dependent effects from mutation-specific responses. Cells expressing high levels of wildtype GFAP increasingly resembled R239C-expressing cells at the proteomic level, supporting a role for GFAP accumulation in disease pathogenesis. In contrast, mutant GFAP specifically correlated with increased mitochondrial antioxidant enzymes and remodeling of the proteasome tailored toward ATP- and ubiquitin-independent degradation of oxidized proteins. Integration with proteomics data from patient-derived cortical organoids revealed increased abundance of nuclear-encoded mitochondrial proteins and respiratory chain assembly factors without corresponding changes in mitochondrially encoded proteins, suggesting impaired electron transport chain (ETC) assembly. Consistent with this, AxD cells displayed reduced oxygen consumption, increased glycolytic activity, enhanced utilization of the cytosolic part of the glycerol-3-phosphate (G3P) shuttle, and accumulation of triacylglycerides and lipid droplets. We propose that ETC dysfunction promotes partial uncoupling of the G3P shuttle, allowing sustained regeneration of cytosolic NAD to support glycolysis while diverting G3P toward lipid synthesis and storage. By regenerating NAD through the G3P shuttle rather than lactate fermentation, AxD astrocytes may sustain glycolytic flux while preserving lactate production, an important metabolic substrate for neighboring neurons. Together, our findings identify ETC dysfunction as a central feature of AxD and suggest that metabolic adaptation through the G3P shuttle links mitochondrial dysfunction to glycolytic reprogramming and lipid accumulation. More broadly, these results provide new insight into how astrocyte dysfunction drives molecular pathways implicated in neurodegeneration.

5-HT1A Antagonist WAY-100635 Prevents Chronic Neurodegeneration and Immune Activation After TBI🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-12 · Das, A., Dutta, S., Pradhan, L. K., Chen, J., Wang, N., Gao, X.
关键词『neurodegeneration』(命中title)
Traumatic brain injury (TBI) initiates secondary neurodegeneration that compromises interconnected neural circuits and contributes to persistent visual and neurobehavioral impairment. Pharmacological strategies that preserve vulnerable neurons and synapses while limiting chronic neuroinflammation remain an unmet therapeutic need. We previously identified the serotonin 1A receptor (5-HT1A) antagonist WAY-100635 through screening human stem cell-derived retinal ganglion cells (RGCs) and demonstrated that it promotes neuroprotection through cyclic adenosine monophosphate-dependent mitochondrial biogenesis. Here, we investigated whether post-injury treatment extends this neuroprotective efficacy to cerebral and visual circuitry following TBI. Male C57BL/6J mice aged 2-3 months underwent unilateral controlled cortical impact (CCI) involving the left visual cortex. Mice were randomly assigned to sham, untreated injury or treated injury groups, with 3-10 sham controls and 5-8 mice per injury group across experiments. WAY-100635 was administered intraperitoneally at 5 mg/kg daily, beginning 1 hour after injury and continuing for 6 weeks. Therapeutic efficacy was assessed using motor and psychological-health-relevant risk-taking/behavioral disinhibition by elevated-plus-maze assessment, visual electrophysiology, optokinetic responses, diffusion magnetic resonance imaging, and retinal and cortical histopathology. Unilateral cortical injury produced bilateral RGC dysfunction and loss, with greater degeneration in the eye contralateral to the injured hemisphere, consistent with predominantly crossed murine visual projections. WAY-100635 significantly preserved RGC survival and function, improved visual acuity and contrast sensitivity, and preserved visually evoked cortical responses to contralateral eye stimulation. Diffusion MRI revealed bilateral visual pathway abnormalities, with treatment selectively normalizing fractional anisotropy and radial kurtosis in the optic tract ipsilateral to cortical injury. In perilesional cortex, treatment preserved neuronal cell bodies and synapses detected by synaptophysin immunoreactivity while markedly reducing microglial/macrophage activation and reactive astrogliosis. These effects were accompanied by improved early motor performance and attenuation of injury-associated increases in open-arm exploration in the elevated plus maze. Cortical cavity volume was not significantly reduced, supporting preferential protection of vulnerable circuitry surrounding and connected to the primary lesion. These findings identify post-injury WAY-100635 treatment as a novel therapeutic intervention that combines neuronal and synaptic preservation with reduced chronic glial activation and improved functional outcomes. They support further translational development of serotonin 1A receptor antagonism to limit secondary neurodegeneration and the visual and neurobehavioral consequences of TBI.

A rapid, genetically-tractable human iPSC-derived model for studying tissue-resident macrophage biology in Alzheimer's and other diseases🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-12 · Lee, H., Peppler, G. B., Sewell, M. … Orrick, J., Marcora, E., Goate, A. M.（共 9 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer、amyloid』(命中title/abstract)
Large-scale genetic and preclinical studies strongly implicate myeloid cells, particularly brain-resident macrophages, in the pathogenesis of Alzheimer's disease (AD). Efferocytic clearance of apoptotic cells and other cholesterol-rich cellular debris is a core macrophage function and represents an active area of AD research. However, human tissue-resident macrophages remain difficult to model in vitro as they acquire their transcriptional and functional identities in response to signals from their host tissue microenvironment. Many existing differentiation protocols mimic human brain macrophage developmental trajectories, but these protocols rely either on multi-step cytokine cocktails and lengthy differentiation or transduction of multiple transcription factors, both of which introduce variability and complicate genetic manipulation. To address these limitations, we developed a simplified protocol that generates high-purity (>90% CD45+CD11b+) human iPSC-derived macrophage-like cells (iMacs) from cryopreservable erythro-myeloid progenitor (EMP)-like intermediates in 19 days, including a final 7-day maturation step driven by macrophage colony-stimulating factor (M-CSF) alone. Transcriptomic profiling reveals that iMacs show enrichment for phagocytic and lysosomal programs, processes integral to efferocytosis. At both the transcriptional and protein level, iMacs have higher expression of scavenger receptors such as CD36, CD163 and MRC1, and of AD risk genes such as TREM2. Functionally, iMacs show enhanced phagocytic uptake of diverse substrates relative to iPSC-derived microglia, including carboxylated latex beads, zymosan, amyloid-{beta} 1-42, and amyloid-{beta} 1-40, and exhibit expanded lysosomal mass. Robust TREM2 expression and signaling make iMacs a sensitive platform for interrogating TREM2 biology, with markedly higher phosphorylation of SYK and PLC{gamma}2 in response to TREM2 engagement compared to iPSC-derived microglia. iMacs also tolerate lipid-mediated siRNA transfection with high survival and knockdown efficiency, enabling rapid genetic perturbation of AD risk genes. Together, these features make iMacs a practical, scalable, and genetically tractable human macrophage model for studying the regulation and cell biology of core macrophage functions that are known to play critical roles in AD, aging, and other disorders.

Cortical projection neurons exhibit early maturation defects and selective degeneration in a mouse model of spinal muscular atrophy🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-11 · Schellino, R., La Rocca, F., Iezzi, G., Caretto, A., Boido, M., Vercelli, A.
关键词『neurodegeneration』(命中abstract)
Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) is usually considered a lower motor neuron disease; however, growing evidence from both experimental models and clinical studies indicates that SMN deficiency affects the whole CNS, beyond the spinal and brainstem motor system. Here, we investigated the temporal progression and selective vulnerability of cortical projection neurons in the SMN{Delta}7 mouse model at early (P5) and late (P11) symptomatic stages. Combining subtype-specific immunohistochemistry, DiI tracing associated with morphometric reconstruction, and dendritic spine analysis, we identified stage- and subtype-dependent cortical alterations. At P11, corticospinal and infragranular callosal projection neurons were selectively reduced in number and showed marked dendritic remodeling and altered excitatory synaptic organization, whereas corticothalamic neurons and supragranular SATB2-positive populations were comparatively preserved. Notably, cortical abnormalities were already detectable at P5, before overt neuronal loss: indeed, corticospinal neurons exhibited reduced soma size and dendritic complexity, while dendritic spine density remained preserved, but spine morphology was shifted toward immature phenotypes. These early maturation defects persisted during disease progression and preceded the reduction in spine density observed at P11. Together, our findings identify cortical projection neurons as selectively vulnerable to constitutive SMN deficiency and suggest that impaired neuronal and synaptic maturation represents an early feature of cortical pathology preceding subtype-specific neurodegeneration. These results expand the pathophysiological spectrum of SMA beyond lower motor neurons and highlight early brain alterations as a potential component of disease progression.

A large, comprehensive labeled MRI dataset of the human lateral ventricle choroid plexus🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-11 · Huang, L.-Y., Zeng, V., Raymond, N. … Estrada, S., Reuter, M., Lizano, P.（共 22 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer』(命中abstract)
The choroid plexus (ChP) is a highly vascularized structure located in the brain ventricles that plays important roles in multiple central nervous system functions. ChP enlargement has been implicated in many neuropsychiatric conditions such as multiple sclerosis, depression, schizophrenia, and Alzheimer's disease. However, limitation in the accuracy of the current segmentation tools highlight the need for more reliable methods to quantify ChP volume in healthy and clinical human populations. The shape and size of the ChP vary drastically from person to person, and currently there are no large ChP datasets which allow comprehensive training of segmentation models. Here we introduce DataPlex, a collection of 700 structural MRI brain images where the lateral ventricle ChP has been manually labeled and checked by expert human raters. DataPlex is balanced and representative across a wide range of scanning parameters, demographics, and clinical profiles, and subject-level information will be available for each labeled image. DataPlex will be open source to support the creation of reliable segmentation tools for the human lateral ventricle ChP.

Continuous amyloid staging complements global amyloid burden in early Alzheimer's disease🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-11 · Liu, H., Tong, M., Fang, S. … Saykin, A. J., Yan, J., For the Alzheimers Disease Neuroimaging Initiative,（共 12 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer、amyloid』(命中title)
Introduction: Early Alzheimer's disease (AD) staging remains challenging because clinical categories and global amyloid PET measures provide only coarse characterization of disease progression. We developed a continuous amyloid staging score from regional amyloid PET to complement global amyloid burden. Methods: Using longitudinal florbetapir PET from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI), we trained an end-to-end deep learning model to directly estimate a continuous amyloid staging score. We evaluated longitudinal consistency, its relationship with global amyloid burden, regional contributors to early-stage variation, and whether the staging score stratified subsequent memory decline among participants matched for baseline global amyloid burden. Cognitive analyses were replicated in A4+LEARN. Results: The staging score better preserved longitudinal visit order than existing amyloid staging approaches. During early disease stages, it captured regional amyloid variation despite relatively stable global amyloid burden. Among participants with similar baseline global amyloid burden, higher staging scores were associated with faster subsequent memory decline in both ADNI and A4+LEARN. Discussion: A continuous amyloid staging score complements global amyloid burden for early AD staging and cognitive risk stratification. Keywords: Alzheimer's disease; amyloid PET; disease staging; cognitive decline

Autonomic neurons are intrinsically hyperexcitable in a Friedreich ataxia iPSC model🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-11 · (Max), S. L., Lees, J., Zhang, H. … McCarthy, D., Napierala, J., Napierala, M.（共 14 位作者）
关键词『neurodegeneration』(命中abstract)
Friedreich ataxia (FRDA) is an inherited multisystem disorder caused by a GAA repeat expansion in the FXN gene and consequent frataxin deficiency, causing progressive neurodegeneration and heart disease. Clinical studies have documented autonomic dysfunction in FRDA, including elevated resting heart rate, raised plasma catecholamines and a high burden of urinary, bowel and sexual symptoms. Consistent with this, autopsy studies show degeneration of the cardiac conduction system and its associated ganglia in FRDA. Whether these features are intrinsic to FRDA autonomic neurons has been untestable without a human cellular model. Here, a human model of FRDA autonomic neurons was established by differentiating induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from individuals with FRDA and from their CRISPR/Cas9-corrected isogenic controls. FRDA autonomic neurons expressed canonical autonomic lineage markers and retained reduced FXN mRNA and frataxin protein levels. Electrophysiological analysis revealed pronounced hyperexcitability, with an approximately five-fold increase in spike number, shortened interspike intervals, and longer bursts. FRDA autonomic neurons also exhibited increased cell death, elevated mitochondrial reactive oxygen species, and a shift from networked to tubular mitochondrial morphology. Bulk RNA sequencing of all three isogenic pairs revealed loss of extracellular matrix programs and activation of stress response pathways. The top differentially expressed gene was MEG3, a maternally imprinted long noncoding RNA, which was consistently upregulated across all three isogenic pairs. MEG3 sits within the imprinted 14q32 DLK1-DIO3 locus, whose maternally expressed noncoding transcripts were co-ordinately de-repressed in FRDA autonomic neurons. Competitive gene-set testing of 43 imprinted genes across 20 domains showed this to be specific to 14q32: no other imprinted domain was co-ordinately altered. Together, these findings establish hyperexcitability of autonomic neurons as a cell autonomous consequence of frataxin deficiency, accompanied by mitochondrial dysfunction. This provides a cellular basis for the increased sympathetic tone observed in clinical studies.
