Chronic evoked seizures differentially worsen cognitive function and long-term neuropsychiatric comorbidities in Alzheimer's disease-related genotypes🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-23 · Knox, K., Fang, J., Davidson, S., Barker-Haliski, M.
关键词『Alzheimer、amyloid』(命中title/abstract)
Variants in one of three deterministic risk genes - amyloid precursor protein (APP), presenilin 1 (PSEN1), or presenilin 2 (PSEN2) - lead to early-onset Alzheimer's disease (EOAD), which also carries a high risk of spontaneous seizures. Seizures may worsen AD-related behavioral comorbidities, including depression, anxiety, and cognitive impairment. Using the corneal kindling model of secondarily generalized focal seizures in two distinct aged male and female EOAD-associated mouse models, we assessed the interaction between investigator-initiated chronic seizures and EOAD genotype on AD-related behavioral comorbidities. Genotypes were selected to model amyloid-b; independent and dependent AD risk factors and included: PSEN2-N141I (amyloid-b; independent model) versus wild-type (PSEN2 control) mice, and APP/PS1 (amyloid-b dependent model) versus wild-type littermates (Tg-). Sham and fully kindled mice then underwent behavioral assessment in the saccharin preference test of anhedonia-like behavior, the Barnes maze test of spatial learning and memory, and a 67-hour home cage monitoring of circadian activity. EOAD genotype only significantly affected kindling acquisition of aged APP/PS1 females. However, there was no significant genotype or kindling effect on saccharin preference, suggesting no adverse impact on anhedonia-like behavior. Cognitive performance in the Barnes maze was significantly worsened by seizures exclusively in APP/PS1 female mice. However, seizures generally worsened Barnes maze performance of male PSEN2-N141I and PSEN2 control mice. Finally, chronic seizures consistently worsened exploratory behavior patterns during home cage circadian activity monitoring exclusively in APP/PS1 male and female mice. This study reveals the AD-related heterogeneity of seizure-evoked behavioral consequences on spatial working memory and circadian behaviors.

Identification of a human Anxa1-associated dopaminergic population linked to Parkinson's disease vulnerability and sensorimotor putaminal networks🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-23 · Schonfeld, E., Baliki, M. N., Monje, M. H. G. … Surmeier, D. J., Dombeck, D. A., Awatramani, R.（共 11 位作者）
关键词『Parkinson』(命中title)
The selective loss of dopaminergic (DA) neurons drives motor symptoms of Parkinson's disease (PD). In mice, Anxa1+ SNc DA neurons are highly vulnerable in PD models, project to the dorsolateral striatum, and display movement-correlated activity, yet they lack clearly defined human counterparts. We transfer the mouse taxonomy to human using a hierarchical mixture-of-experts model, recovering a human Anxa1-associated population. We find that this was the most depleted subtype in PD brains. A ligand-receptor algorithm, validated against mouse tracing, infers that these neurons preferentially innervate the caudal putamen. There, DaT-SPECT shows the earliest and most severe DA axon loss in PD. Functional connectivity positions this region as a critical node within the somato-cognitive action network, linking DA neuron vulnerability to circuit dysfunction. To translate these insights, we present an interactive portal unifying human datasets with a cross-species FDA Drug Atlas, nominating CACNA1G as a candidate neuroprotective target for the Anxa1-associated population.

A latent tau amplifier in the neuronal proteome lowers the seeding threshold for Alzheimers disease pathology🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-22 · Tsaka, G., Kolla, R., Wiedmer, A. E. … Thal, D. R., Rousseau, F., Schymkowitz, J.（共 19 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer、tau』(命中title)
The stereotyped spread of tau pathology through connected brain regions is accompanied by striking differences in the vulnerability of individual neuronal populations, pointing to cell-intrinsic determinants of susceptibility. Using a combination of computational APR analysis, in vitro and cellular aggregation assays, and quantitative proteomics of human postmortem brain, we identify the splicing factor TRA2B as an endogenous modifier of this susceptibility. TRA2B enhances the activity of patient-derived tau seeds, promotes tau pathology in vivo, and progressively redistributes from the nucleus into insoluble tau-associated material in Alzheimers disease brain. Mechanistically, TRA2B contains two aggregation-prone regions (APRs) homologous to tau nucleation sites that are required for tau interaction and seeding enhancement. Yet this activity is not constitutive: isolated TRA2B APRs promote aggregation, whereas the full-length protein can inhibit, amplify or have little effect on tau aggregation depending on protein state and seeding context. Perturbation of the regulatory RS2 region switches TRA2B away from its amplifying state, revealing that its sequence-encoded aggregation potential is regulatable. Thus, TRA2B acts as a latent tau amplifier, providing a mechanism by which the neuronal proteome can tune cellular susceptibility to the propagation of pathological tau.

The exocyst is an insulin-sensitive regulator of amyloid precursor protein trafficking and amyloid-beta generation in neurons🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-22 · Balaan, C., Patwardhan, G. Y., Sachs, R. K. … Ortega, M. A., Nichols, R. A., Fogelgren, B.（共 14 位作者）
关键词『amyloid』(命中title)
Intracellular trafficking of amyloid precursor protein (APP) critically influences amyloidogenic processing, yet the mechanisms regulating this pathway remain incompletely defined. The exocyst is a highly conserved, insulin-responsive, eight-protein Rab effector complex that directs intracellular transport vesicle targeting and docking. We identified APP in a proteomics screen of neuronal cell surface proteins altered following chemical inhibition of exocyst activity. In SH-SY5Y cells expressing an APP mutant that enhances amyloidogenic processing, RNAi-mediated silencing of exocyst subunits significantly decreased sAPP and A{beta} secretion, accompanied by intracellular APP accumulation. High-resolution microscopy revealed APP colocalization with exocyst subunits in the soma and neurites of differentiated SH-SY5Y neurons and primary mouse hippocampal neurons. Live-cell TIRF microscopy demonstrated coordinated movement of fluorescently tagged exocyst and APP proteins. Proximity ligation assays (PLAs) confirmed close (<40nm) APP-EXOC5 association in differentiated SH-SY5Y neurons, primary mouse hippocampal neurons, and histological sections of mouse brain. To examine whether insulin regulates neuronal exocyst activity, as in adipocytes and muscle, we generated SH-SY5Y cells expressing pHluorin-tagged GLUT4. Using this model, we found that chemical inhibition of exocyst activity prevented insulin-stimulated GLUT4 exocytosis to the plasma membrane. Furthermore, PLAs in both primary hippocampal and SH-SY5Y neurons revealed that insulin increased GLUT4-EXOC5 associations while markedly reducing APP-EXOC5 associations. These findings suggest that insulin signaling redirects the exocyst away from APP-positive vesicles towards GLUT-positive vesicles. Together, our data identify the exocyst as a novel insulin-regulated mediator of neuronal APP trafficking and A{beta} secretion.
