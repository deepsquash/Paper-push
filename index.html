40 Hz visual stimulation increases gamma-band functional connectivity between visual, parietal and frontal regions🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-05 · Ma, T., Sun, Q., Mlinaric, T., Van Hulle, M. M.
关键词『Alzheimer』(命中abstract)
Visual stimulation at 40 Hz is being investigated as a non-invasive intervention for Alzheimer's disease. Its neural responses have been reported in regions beyond the visual cortex, and concurrent cognitive tasks have been shown to increase their spatial extent. However, it is unclear whether these responses reflect increased functional connectivity between regions or a common response to the stimulus, and whether a concurrent task changes this connectivity. We recorded EEG from 29 healthy young adults during Regular 40 Hz flicker and Irregular 30-50 Hz flicker, each with and without a target-counting task (2 x 2 within-participant design). Source-space connectivity between visual, parietal, and frontal regions was estimated with the debiased squared weighted phase-lag index (dWPLI). Regular stimulation increased 38-42 Hz connectivity for all three region pairs, with the largest increase between visual and parietal regions (increase in all 29 participants). The increase was specific to the stimulation frequency and was observed with two source reconstruction methods and in sensor space. The visual-parietal increase remained significant after correction for source leakage (spatial mixing between reconstructed sources) and after removal of the estimated stimulus-locked component, whereas the increases in visual-frontal and parietal-frontal connectivity did not. The concurrent task did not modulate the visual-parietal effect. For visual-frontal and parietal-frontal connectivity, the stimulation-by-ask interaction was not significant. These results show that 40 Hz visual stimulation increases gamma-band connectivity beyond the visual cortex, and much of this increase reflects a common stimulus-locked response, and connectivity beyond this response was observed mainly between visual and parietal regions.

Disrupted sleep-wake regulation in the MCI-Park mouse model of Parkinson's Disease🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-05 · Summa, K. C., Jiang, P., Gonzalez-Rodriguez, P. … Dan, Y., Surmeier, D. J., Turek, F. W.（共 9 位作者）
关键词『Parkinson』(命中title)
Disrupted sleep has a profound adverse impact on lives of Parkinson's Disease (PD) patients and their caregivers. Sleep disturbances are exceedingly common in PD, with substantial heterogeneity in type, timing, and severity. Among the most common sleep-related symptoms reported by PD patients are insomnia, excessive daytime sleepiness, and sleep fragmentation, characterized by interruptions and decreased continuity of sleep. Alterations in brain wave activity, as measured on the electroencephalogram (EEG), also occur in PD, with changes in the pattern and relative contributions of different frequency bands of the EEG spectrum to overall EEG activity in different vigilance states consistently observed. The mechanisms underlying these PD-associated sleep-wake abnormalities are poorly understood, and they are ineffectively treated by conventional PD therapies. To help fill this gap in knowledge, a new progressive model of PD - the MCI-Park mouse- was studied. Near the transition to the parkinsonian state, these mice exhibited significantly altered sleep-wake regulation, including increased wakefulness, decreased non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep, increased sleep fragmentation, reduced rapid eye movement (REM) sleep, and altered EEG activity patterns. These sleep-wake abnormalities mirror those identified in PD patients. Thus, this model may help elucidate the circuit mechanisms underlying sleep disruption in PD and identify targets for novel therapeutic approaches.

Individualized AI-driven Neuromodulation Selectively Enhances and Reorganizes Human Tongue Sensorimotor Networks.🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-05 · Papageorgiou, T. D., Webb, J., Allam, A. … Thompson, A. M., Neblett, C. R., Froudarakis, E.（共 15 位作者）
关键词『amyotrophic lateral sclerosis』(命中abstract)
Precise modulation of brain networks for tongue motor and sensory control (TMSC) is critical for neruorehabilitation. Existing neuromodulation approaches use fixed targets, ignoring individual network architecture. We present an AI-driven, individualized fMRI neuromodulation (iNM) platform adaptively targeting subject-specific TMSC networks in real-time. Using a neurofunctional twin built from each participant's anatomy and function, iNM reinforces targeted activity and suppresses competing states. Unsupervised machine learning and hidden Markov modeling showed iNM increased TMSC-state occupancy from 82% to 97% and suppressed swallow-state interference by 83%. The inferior parietal lobule emerged as a sensorimotor integration hub, with increased magnitude (d' 90.4 right, 84 left) and spatial expansion (134.8% right, 81.8% left). Cortico-subcortical connectivity strengthened 53% to 74% across motor, sensory, and attention networks and persisted into non-iNM blocks. Thus, iNM may offer precision neurorehabilitation for CNS or PNS disorders, such as amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and treatment-induced cranial neuropathy in head-and-neck-cancer survivors, respectively.

Sex-specific gene and isoform expression characterizes hippocampal aging in rhesus macaques🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-05 · Harper-Alexander, V., Peterson, S., Sadikova, S., Kohama, S. G., Ram, D. R.
关键词『Alzheimer』(命中abstract)
Aging produces widespread transcriptomic alterations that extend beyond changes in gene expression to include changes at the isoform level. Declining splicing fidelity, including increased intron retention and aberrant isoform expression, has been proposed to contribute to cellular dysfunction; however, the extent and biological significance of isoform-specific changes in the aging brain remain poorly understood. Age-related cognitive decline has also been associated with neuroinflammation and the development of a chronic inflammatory state, or ''inflammaging,'' within the brain. Here, we used short- and long-read RNA sequencing to characterize gene- and isoform-level changes associated with hippocampal aging across sex in 24 rhesus macaques. We identified 594 age-associated differential isoform expression events, comprising both shared and sex-specific changes. Inflammatory pathways, including TNF- -- NF-{kappa}B signaling, interferon responses, and complement pathways, were enriched across both sexes. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) further identified sex-specific modules associated with inflammatory, glial, mitochondrial, and hormone-responsive processes. Notably, several age-associated changes were detected at the isoform level despite limited or absent corresponding gene-level differences, highlighting transcript-level regulation as an additional dimension of brain aging that may be overlooked by gene-level analyses. To investigate the relationship between aging and Alzheimer's disease (AD), we independently performed WGCNA on the rhesus macaque cohort and a publicly available human hippocampal AD single-nucleus RNA-sequencing dataset (GSE268609). Cross-species module preservation and gene overlap analyses identified shared genes between age-associated and AD-associated networks, with pathway enrichment implicating glial function, inflammation, and mitochondrial biology. Together, these findings highlight sex-associated differences in hippocampal transcriptomic aging, underscore the importance of isoform-level regulation, and identify shared transcriptional signatures between normal aging and AD-associated pathways.

Use of Integrative Proteomic Analysis to Uncover Shared Molecular Signatures Across Distinct Forms of Hydrocephalus🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-05 · Mahendran, G., Kirupaharan, P., Audu, D., Blazer-Yost, B. L.
关键词『neurodegeneration』(命中abstract)
Hydrocephalus is a heterogeneous disease characterized by disruption of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) dynamics and can arise from diverse etiologies, including post-hemorrhagic hydrocephalus (PHH), hydrocephalus following subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH), post-infectious hydrocephalus (PIH), and idiopathic normal pressure hydrocephalus (iNPH). Hydrocephalus has high disease burden in pediatric and older populations and a marked increase in prevalence with advancing age. Despite this high incidence, the underpinning molecular and signaling pathway alterations remain incompletely understood. Hence CSF proteomic profiling would enable a robust and high-resolution approach for exploring disease-specific biomarkers and to discover shared molecular mechanisms underlying hydrocephalus. In this study, we performed a cross-study proteomic analysis to evaluate biomarker proteins reported across PHH, SAH, PIH, and iNPH groups. Using published proteomic datasets, we comparatively analyzed CSF proteins to investigate the differentially expressed proteins (DEPs), their common molecular signatures, and enriched biological pathways across hydrocephalus groups. Across etiologies, recurrently altered proteins were predominantly associated with inflammatory signaling (cytokine, chemokine, interleukin-(IL-17), tumor necrosis factor (TNF)), toll-like receptor (TLR) signaling, blood-brain-barrier (BBB) disruption leading to coagulation cascades, and neuronal injury responses. Hemorrhage-associated forms of hydrocephalus (PHH and SAH) demonstrated enriched coagulation and hemoglobin-related pathways, whereas infection associated forms (PIH) showed enhanced immune and inflammatory responses. iNPH exhibited alterations in proteins linked to neurodegeneration, synaptic function, and impaired CSF clearance. Interestingly, our cross-study analyses identified recurrently dysregulated proteins across hydrocephalus subtypes. Collectively, our cross-study proteomic analyses define shared and etiology-specific proteomic signatures across hydrocephalus subtypes and identify potential CSF-based biomarkers for diagnostic stratification and future precision therapeutic approaches.

Conserved synaptic modifications by humanized tau through diverse isoform- and mutation-specific intracellular interactions🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-04 · Nickerson, E., Pomeroy-Tuck, J., Pratt, S. … Hoffman, B., Maser, R., Bloss, E.（共 9 位作者）
关键词『tau』(命中title)
Mutations in the microtubule-binding protein tau are considered causal for a diverse group of neurodegenerative diseases collectively referred to as primary tauopathies. Specific tauopathies are associated with different mutations, distinct tau isoforms, manifest in unique cellular and circuit-wide patterns of degeneration, and present with specific clinical symptoms and trajectories. The mechanisms that link variations in the primary tau protein structure to distinct forms of neural circuit dysfunction remain largely unknown. Here, we created an orthogonal viral resource to systematically examine the impact of expressing humanized tau isoforms harboring different disease-relevant mutations in excitatory pyramidal cells from the mouse prelimbic cortex. We used these reagents to show that expression of mutant humanized tau (hTau) as either of two isoforms produced very similar patterns of circuit-specific synaptic disconnection on cortical projection neurons. However, mass spectrometry revealed that the interactomes of these different tau species varied markedly as functions of isoform, mutation, and subcellular location. We validated the functional relevance of increased hTau interactions with mitochondrial proteins in induced neurons differentiated from Ngn2-iPSCs expressing different hTau constructs. These results suggest different disease-relevant hTau proteins likely use distinct mechanisms to drive a highly stereotyped form of synapse loss in cortical neurons.

Chronic Sleep Disruption Activates the Transcriptional Unfolded Protein Response and Induces Compartment-Specific Proteomic Changes in Mice🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-04 · Espinosa-Garcia, C., Kour, D., Srivastava, U. … Wood, L. B., Faundez, V., Rangaraju, S.（共 16 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer』(命中abstract)
Sleep disruption (SD) is associated with adverse brain health and increased risk of dementia, including Alzheimer's disease (AD), yet the pathological mechanisms underlying this association are not fully understood. Preclinical studies have shown that acute SD impairs cognition by altering vulnerable synapses (molecular composition, ultrastructure, and function). However, the cumulative effects of chronic SD on the adult brain and its vulnerable synapses, that might contribute to AD risk, remain poorly defined. Here, we investigated the impact of chronic SD on brain transcriptomic signatures and subcellular compartment-specific proteomic profiles to identify genes, proteins, and signaling pathways that might confer AD risk. Chronic SD induced a modest transcriptional stress response globally, whereas alterations were more pronounced in the synaptosomal (synapse-enriched P2 fractions) and whole-brain proteomes. Remarkably, chronic SD elicited unique proteomic alterations at the synaptic compartment were distinct from whole-brain effects, including increased proteins involved in synaptic vesicle endocytosis, regulation of dendritic spine morphogenesis, and regulation of long-term synaptic depression, compared to increased proteins involved in immune/inflammatory, glial responses and neuronal function at the whole-brain level. Consistent with the chronic SD-induced molecular signatures, we observed subtle ultrastructural changes in vulnerable CA1 hippocampal synapses, including reduced pre-synapse size and decreased synaptic vesicle density, which suggest that chronic SD modifies synaptic transmission and plasticity. Together, our findings indicate that chronic SD differentially impacts the brain and the synaptic proteome, nominating candidate therapeutic targets and signaling pathways for future mechanistic studies of chronic SD and how SD contributes to increased AD risk.

FGF signaling induces disease-associated oligodendrocyte states and drives myelin pathology🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-04 · Weigel, M. K., Cooper, M. H., Zaghi, M. … Steinman, L., Castelo-Branco, G., Zuchero, J. B.（共 22 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer』(命中abstract)
Myelin integrity is essential for normal central nervous system (CNS) function and is disrupted in neuroinflammatory and neurodegenerative disorders, including multiple sclerosis and Alzheimer's disease. In these settings, myelin-forming oligodendrocytes adopt a conserved disease-associated transcriptional state, termed disease-associated oligodendrocytes (DAOs). How DAOs arise, the signals that drive their induction, and whether they actively contribute to pathology remain poorly understood. Here we show that FGF signaling instructs mature oligodendrocytes to enter disease-associated states that are sufficient to trigger myelin pathology. By screening disease-linked secreted factors, we identify FGF1 and FGF2 as potent inducers of a DAO-like state in purified oligodendrocyte cultures, characterized by transcriptional reprogramming and myelin-membrane shedding. In vivo, activating oligodendrocyte FGF signaling is sufficient to elicit DAO programs and myelin disruption, whereas inhibiting FGF signaling suppresses DAO induction in a mouse model of multiple sclerosis. Finally, analysis of post-mortem multiple sclerosis tissue reveals a concordant DAO-like state associated with increased engagement of the FGF/FGFR axis. Collectively, these studies define a regulated mechanism of DAO formation linked to myelin disruption and position mature oligodendrocytes as active contributors to myelin pathology in CNS disease.

Lipidome of Compact Myelin from Cortical Regions of the Aging Murine Brain🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-04 · Liu, V. P., Johnson, C. E., Lakhani, S., Furber, K. L.
关键词『neurodegeneration』(命中abstract)
Age-related cognitive decline has been associated with neurodegeneration in cortical white matter tracts. The trajectory of white matter degeneration over the lifespan follows a similar pattern in humans, primates and rodents including the loss of myelin integrity and concomitant loss in myelinated axonal segments. Previous studies suggest that biochemical alterations in myelin begin to occur well prior to myelin loss. To better understand these biochemical determinants underlying white matter degeneration, we investigated the lipid composition of myelin isolated from young adult, middle-aged and aged murine brain. Compact myelin was enriched from cortical regions using a refined density centrifugation protocol and profiled the lipidome using LC-MS/MS. Over 2000 lipid species were identified, with the relative expression of 414 species changing over the age-span. The myelin lipidome from young mice was biochemically distinct compared to the aging cohorts. Aging was characterized by an overall decrease in high abundant species of glycerophospholipid and sphingolipids, including phosphocholine, phosphoethanolamine, sphingomyelin and cerebrosides. Pathway analysis by LipidSig 2.0 and lipid ontology analysis by LION/web highlighted alterations to lipid metabolism and membrane biophysical properties related to bilayer stability and organization. These data support the concept that myelin lipid compositional changes are apparent by middle age which underly structural changes in myelin at later ages. The findings provide greater insight into age-related biochemical alterations in the myelin sheath and may aid in strategies to mitigate white matter degeneration over the lifespan.

Interpretable Machine Learning Links Group-Specific Plasma Proteomics to Regional Structural Neuroimaging in Alzheimer's Disease🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-04 · Laugi, H., Hosseinzadehketilateh, M., Lee, S., Weisberg, S. M., Zhu, X.
关键词『Alzheimer、neurodegeneration』(命中title/abstract)
Background: Alzheimer's disease (AD) pathology begins years before clinical symptoms, highlighting the need for scalable biomarkers that capture biological changes across the AD continuum. Broader plasma proteomics may capture systemic processes that vary with disease stage, yet their relationship to AD-related brain neurodegeneration remains unclear. We aimed to identify plasma proteomic signatures across cognitively normal (CN), mild cognitive impairment (MCI), and AD and evaluate their neuroanatomical associations. Methods: We analyzed 146 plasma analytes from 565 Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) participants (58 CN, 396 MCI, 111 AD). Group-specific proteomic signatures were identified using pairwise classification models with nested 5*5 cross-validation. SHAP quantified feature importance, with the top 15 proteins per comparison being prioritized for MRI association analysis. We analyzed 103 regional structural MRI volumes harmonized across 57 acquisition sites using ComBat. Separate ordinary least squares regression tested protein-MRI associations, adjusting for age, sex, APOE4, diagnosis, and intracranial volume, followed by Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction. Results: Classification models using proteomic data achieved AUC of 0.956 +- 0.017 for CN vs MCI, 0.963 +- 0.011 for MCI vs. AD, and 0.836 +- 0.085 for CN vs. AD. SHAP identified group-dependent proteomic signatures. No protein-MRI associations survived FDR correction for CN vs. MCI. In MCI vs. AD, higher cortisol was associated with lower left precuneus and superior parietal volumes, while higher osteopontin was associated with lower left paracentral and accumbens volumes. In CN vs. AD, higher vitronectin was associated with lower posterior corpus callosum volume. Conclusion: These findings suggest that plasma proteomics capture group-specific systemic biological signals across the AD continuum. Linking ML-prioritized plasma proteins to regional brain structure provides neuroanatomical context for blood-based biomarker discovery.

Integrative Transcriptomic Meta-Analysis Reveals SIRT1-SNCA Correlation in Parkinson's Disease🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-04 · Cimen, H., Sonmez, E., Hotaslier, F.
关键词『Parkinson』(命中title)
Parkinson's disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder that affects midbrain dopaminergic neurons without any approved therapy. Candidate molecular targets identified in one model are not always traceable across the postmortem, induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC), and cell-based systems used to study PD, which complicates clinical translation. To address this gap, we performed an integrative transcriptomic meta-analysis of 33 publicly available datasets across postmortem human brain, iPSC-derived models, and dopaminergic cell lines of PD. Differential expression and functional enrichment analyses were combined with STRING protein-protein interaction networks, followed by drug-gene interaction screening against a polyphenol panel. Overall, resveratrol was recovered in all 33 comparisons, independent of model system, tissue, and genetic background. We associated resveratrol with 30 distinct target genes, and SIRT1 was itself a recovered target in 6 comparisons spanning all three model systems. Finally, we resolved the SIRT1-SNCA relationship into four directional patterns that differed by model, inverse in postmortem brain and positive in acutely toxin-exposed cells. These findings support a candidate, context-dependent resveratrol-SIRT1 axis in PD.

Disrupted mannosidase enzymes in C9orf72 ALS/FTD🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-04 · Ondatje, B. N., Gittings, L. M., Preller, K. R. … Pirrotte, P., Van Keuren-Jensen, K., Sattler, R.（共 18 位作者）
关键词『amyotrophic lateral sclerosis、neurodegeneration』(命中abstract)
Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) is an age-related neurodegenerative disorder resulting from the loss of motor neurons in the brain and spinal cord. Neuroinflammation, induced by glial cells including microglia (MG) and astrocytes, is an acknowledged aspect of this neurodegeneration. Generating induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) neuroinflammatory models of ALS may help elucidate non-neuronal mechanisms of disease. Here, we analyze human postmortem tissue and an iPSC derived glia-crosstalk model identifying a reduction in Golgi enzymes MAN1A1 and MAN1A2 in addition to other glycan enzymes from ALS-associated microglia and astrocytes. Impaired Golgi and glycan metabolism is linked to inflammation and other related ALS disease mechanisms including impaired lysosomal proteostasis, altered extracellular matrix, disrupted secretion, and altered cell-cell signaling.

Monoaminergic changes are associated with impaired neurogenesis along the longitudinal axis of the hippocampus in ageing and dementia🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-04 · Mathews, K. J., Abdeen, A. H., Mor, D. … Dalton, M. A., Kwok, J., Double, K. L.（共 7 位作者）
关键词『Parkinson』(命中abstract)
Neurogenesis persists in the adult human hippocampus and is thought to support memory and emotional regulation. Both normal ageing and Parkinson disease dementia (PDD) are associated with reduced hippocampal neurogenesis, but why this occurs and how this might change across the across the hippocampal axis remains unclear. Here, we quantified the expression of genes regulating neurogenesis from stem cell production to neuron maturation and examined their relationship with monoaminergic activity throughout the post-mortem hippocampus in young and aged individuals, as well as in individuals with PDD. DCX and MKI67 expression were markedly reduced in PDD cases throughout the hippocampus, while GFAP and GFAP{delta} were elevated in aged control individuals but not in PDD. Dopamine and serotonin levels were unchanged but reduced levels of the dopamine metabolite DOPAC, and increased levels of the serotonin metabolite 5-HIAA, were present across the hippocampal axis in PDD. Hierarchical regression analysis identified DOPAC and 5-HIAA as significant predictors of DCX and MKI67 expression independent of diagnostic group. Coupling between stem cell markers and proliferative output was preserved in the hippocampus of young controls but was absent in aged controls and in PDD. These data suggest changes in monoaminergic systems are linked to stage-specific deficits in neuronal proliferation and maturation, identifying a potential mechanistic bridge to cognitive decline in PDD.
