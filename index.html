Region-dependent regulation of Tau phosphorylation by Glycerophosphodiester Phosphodiesterase 2 in a mouse model of tauopathy🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-03 · Jimenez-Ornelas, C., Sockanathan, S.
关键词『Alzheimer、tau、neurodegeneration』(命中abstract/title)
Hyperphosphorylation of Tau promotes its aggregation and neurofibrillary tangle (NFT) formation, contributing to neuronal dysfunction and neurodegeneration in diseases such as Alzheimer's Disease (AD) and AD-related dementias (ADRDs). However, the mechanisms underlying dysregulated Tau phosphorylation under pathological contexts remain unclear. Glycerophosphodiester phosphodiesterase 2 (GDE2) is a six-transmembrane enzyme that acts at the cell surface to cleave the glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchor that tethers a subclass of proteins to the membrane. Here, we show that in the PS19 tauopathy mouse model, GDE2 disruption modulates Tau phosphorylation by transiently decreasing Tau's propensity for aggregation through region-specific regulation of local kinase environments. In the cortex, GDE2 ablation in 6-month-old PS19 male mice (PS19;Gde2KO) reduces Tau phosphorylation at Serine (S)202/Threonine (T)205, T212, and S396 but increases S262 phosphorylation in the hippocampus of 6-month-old PS19;Gde2KO male and female mice. Consistent with these changes, in males, Glycogen Synthase Kinase-3 / {beta} (GSK3 / {beta}) activity is decreased in the cortex, while AKT activity is increased in both males and females in the hippocampus, with no changes in protein phosphatase 1 (PP1) and protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A) activity. Primary cortical neurons from PS19;Gde2KO animals showed reduced Tau phosphorylation at S202/T205, implying cell-autonomous roles for neuronal GDE2 in this process. GDE2 overexpression in heterologous SH-SY5Y cells increased Tau phosphorylation at S202/T205, while a catalytically inactive form of GDE2 did not, suggesting that GDE2 regulation of target GPI-anchored protein surface activity is required to modulate Tau phosphorylation. Taken together, our study identifies GDE2 as a component of the complex regulatory network that controls Tau phosphorylation in the context of tauopathy and provides insight into putative pathways relevant to Tau pathologies observed in disease.

Integrated metabolomics and proteomics from voxelated cortical hemispheres of adult rhesus monkeys🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-03 · Wu, Q., Brigande, A. M., Lutz, M. W., Shi, P., Disney, A.
关键词『Alzheimer』(命中abstract)
The spatial organization of molecular networks across cortex likely contributes to differences in local circuit vulnerability in aging and Alzheimer's disease; yet many existing molecular datasets sacrifice spatial structure, sampling only a handful of regions per brain. Here, we present a framework for generating spatially registered, paired metabolomic and proteomic maps across an entire cortical hemisphere of an adult rhesus monkey, at millimeter resolution. One hemisphere each from two animals was harvested under controlled conditions, approximately flattened, and hand dissected at different sampling resolutions (roughly 2.5 and 4 mm/side) into tissue voxels. Each voxel was split after homogenization and extraction to provide matched aliquots for targeted metabolomics and deep untargeted proteomics. To handle these high dimensional data, we developed PChclust, a principal component guided feature clustering algorithm. For cross omic integration, we developed a spatially regularized sparse canonical correlation analysis (sr-sCCA), which incorporates spatial neighborhood structure via graph Laplacian smoothing. We recover meaningful biology: Molecular similarity between neighboring voxels decayed with distance in both modalities, confirming that voxelation captures spatially organized biological variance. The sr-sCCA identified joint proteome-metabolome components with coherent cortical gradients that were conserved across animals. Pathway enrichment analysis recovered brain relevant ontologies and reconstructed complete metabolic circuits from single voxels.

BundleMorphometry: Spatially Resolved White Matter Bundle Shape Analysis Using Nonlinear Streamline-based Registration🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-03 · Dasanayake, D., Amit, S. N., Chandio, B. Q.
关键词『Parkinson』(命中abstract)
Diffusion MRI tractometry quantifies microstructural properties along white matter (WM) bundles but does not directly measure their geometry, potentially overlooking localized morphological alterations. Here, we introduce BundleMorphometry, a framework for quantifying local WM bundle shape using the analytical Jacobian of nonlinear streamline-based registration. Individual subject bundles are registered to a reference atlas bundle using BundleWarp, a streamline-based nonlinear registration method. We derive the analytical Jacobian of the resulting deformation field and compute its log-determinant (log det J) to quantify local expansion and compression at each streamline point. To enable spatially resolved group comparisons, these measurements are projected onto the two-dimensional grid, capturing variations along and across each bundle. We evaluated BundleMorphometry using two clinical datasets comprising patients with glioma and Parkinson's disease (PD). In the glioma dataset, BundleMorphometry revealed spatially extensive deformation patterns in lesion-affected WM bundles that were not captured by displacement magnitude alone. Additionally, in a group analysis of the PD dataset (32 patients and 32 healthy controls), we identified significant localized morphological differences in the corpus callosum forceps major, mid-body and right spinothalamic tract, with distinct patterns of expansion and compression. These findings demonstrate the potential of BundleMorphometry to advance conventional tractometry by enabling spatially resolved analysis of bundle morphology.

关键词『amyotrophic lateral sclerosis』(命中abstract)
Background. Nuclear TDP-43 depletion is a central pathological feature of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and frontotemporal dementia (FTD), but donor-to-donor variation can obscure transcriptomic associations in small postmortem cohorts. We reanalyzed public RNA-seq data from FACS-isolated NeuN-positive frontal-cortex neuronal nuclei to quantify gene-expression changes associated with nuclear TDP-43 loss while explicitly accounting for donor structure. Methods. The supplied GSE126542-derived matrix contained 62,754 genes across 12 libraries from seven donors, including five complete matched pairs. Source labels TDP-43 positive and TDP-43 negative were harmonized as nuclear TDP-43 retained and lost, respectively. After validation against featureCounts-assigned read counts, 44,225 genes with at least 10 total counts were analyzed using PyDESeq2. The primary model was ~ donor + nuclear_status; status-only and diagnosis-adjusted models were sensitivity analyses. The contrast was TDP-43 loss minus retained. Results. The donor-aware model identified 3,442 genes at false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05: 1,865 lower and 1,577 higher in TDP-43-loss nuclei. STMN2 was consistently depleted (log2 fold change = -1.813, 95% Wald interval -2.229 to -1.396; FDR = 6.22e-15), corresponding to approximately 71.5% lower expression. Normalized STMN2 counts were lower in TDP-43-loss nuclei in all five complete donor pairs. TARDBP expression was also lower (log2 fold change = -0.606; FDR = 4.98e-6). In contrast, UNC13A whole-gene expression was modestly higher in the donor-aware model (log2 fold change = 0.372; FDR = 0.00622) and was not significant in either sensitivity model. Conclusions. Donor-aware analysis supports a robust association between nuclear TDP-43 loss and STMN2 depletion in this neuronal-nuclei cohort, alongside extensive but model-dependent transcriptomic variation. Whole-gene UNC13A expression cannot be used to infer UNC13A cryptic-exon inclusion or functional UNC13A depletion. No splice-junction, percent-spliced-in, isoform, or protein measurements were available; mechanistic claims therefore require junction-level and experimental validation.

bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-03 · Parmar, R., Meredith, J. R., Bartlett, M. J. … Chattree, G., Steece-Collier, K., Falk, T.（共 9 位作者）
关键词『Parkinson』(命中abstract)
L-DOPA remains the most effective symptomatic treatment for Parkinson's disease, but chronic administration is frequently complicated by L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia (LID). Our prior work has established that sub-anesthetic ketamine can reduced LID acutely and has a long-lasting neuroplastic effect. This study tested whether low-dose ketamine reduces dyskinesia induced specifically by dopamine D2R-like or D1R-like receptor activation. Male Sprague Dawley rats were primed with daily L-DOPA to establish dyskinesia and then challenged with quinpirole, a D2R-like receptor agonist, or SKF-81297, a D1R-like receptor agonist, to induce abnormal involuntary movements (AIMs). After stable AIM baselines were established, animals received agonist plus vehicle or agonist plus ketamine. Limb, axial, and orolingual AIMs were scored on the acute treatment day (D0) and after washout on D4 and D7. Quinpirole and SKF-81297 reliably produced stable total LAO AIM scores following L-DOPA priming. Ketamine significantly reduced only quinpirole-induced, but not SKF-81297-induced, total AIMs compared with vehicle on D0, demonstrating an acute anti-dyskinetic effect. This reduction persisted after washout, with ketamine-treated animals continuing to show significantly lower AIMs on D4 and D7. Time-course analyses further showed reduced AIMs expression across the post-quinpirole observation period on D0 and at multiple time points on D7. These findings demonstrate that ketamine suppresses quinpirole-induced dyskinesia in L-DOPA-primed male rats and suggest that D2R-like receptor-mediated or indirect pathway-related mechanisms, but not direct pathway-related mechanisms, may contribute to ketamine's anti-dyskinetic effects. Together, these results extend prior work on ketamine by linking its sustained anti-dyskinetic effects to dyskinesia driven by D2R-like receptor stimulation in vivo.

关键词『Parkinson』(命中title)
Biological sex is an important determinant of neuropharmacological response, yet females remain underrepresented in preclinical studies of Parkinson's disease (PD) and L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia (LID). We previously demonstrated that sub-anesthetic ketamine reduces established LID, attenuates dyskinesia development, and produces anti-parkinsonian effects in male unilateral 6-hydroxydopamine (6-OHDA) rat models. However, whether these therapeutic effects extend to females remained unknown. Here, we evaluated the anti-dyskinetic and anti-parkinsonian effects of sub-anesthetic ketamine in female Sprague-Dawley rats using the attenuation and established models of LID. In the attenuation paradigm, unilateral 6-OHDA-lesioned females received repeated L-DOPA administration with either ketamine (20 mg/kg, i.p.; 10-h dosing paradigm) or vehicle. Ketamine significantly attenuated the progressive development of dyskinesia, indicating that early intervention modified the trajectory of LID expression. In the established LID paradigm, ketamine produced robust significant acute reductions in abnormal involuntary movements (AIMs) after dyskinesia had fully developed, and these significant effects persisted for up to 49 days following treatment, demonstrating sustained suppression of dyskinesia severity. To determine whether ketamine altered therapeutic motor function, forelimb adjusting steps testing was performed in female unilateral 6-OHDA rats. Ketamine did not impair L-DOPA-mediated motor improvement suggesting compatibility with the gold standard medication. Ketamine was also by itself associated with significantly improved stepping performance during ketamine treatment conditions, indicating acute anti-parkinsonian motor benefit. Together, these findings demonstrate that the anti-dyskinetic and anti-parkinsonian effects of ketamine extend to female preclinical models and strengthen the translational rationale for repurposing sub-anesthetic ketamine as a potential therapeutic strategy for individuals with LID.

CD40 ligand and IFN-γ induce proinflammatory activity and microglia-mediated neurodegeneration in human stem cell models🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-03 · Hammond, T., Wirak, G. S., Ryan, S. K. … Gandhi, R., Djukic, B., Ofengeim, D.（共 16 位作者）
关键词『neurodegeneration』(命中title)
The cluster of differentiation 40 (CD40)/CD40 ligand (CD40L) co-stimulatory pathway plays a critical role in the activation and regulation of immune networks involving both adaptive and innate immune cells. Dysregulation of this pathway has been implicated in multiple autoimmune diseases, including multiple sclerosis (MS). MS is characterized by progressive neurodegeneration driven by compartmentalized neuroinflammation within the central nervous system (CNS), sustained low-grade T cell infiltration at the blood-brain barrier, and T cell reactivation at CNS border tissues. CD40L-expressing T cells can activate CD40-expressing microglia, which are a critical effector cell that contribute to neurodegeneration in MS. However, the consequence of this functional interaction in the brain remains poorly understood. Here, we demonstrate that the combination of CD40L and interferon-gamma (IFN-{gamma}), a cytokine produced by activated T cells, induces a robust inflammatory response and cell death in human induced pluripotent stem cells (hiPSC)-derived microglia. Stimulation with CD40L elicited microglial clustering, morphological transformation, and robust release of proinflammatory cytokines including interleukin-12 (IL-12), tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-), and IL-6. Anti-CD40L antibody treatment reversed both cell death and inflammatory response and partially normalized the associated transcriptional changes. Furthermore, using an hiPSC-derived tri-culture system comprising neurons, astrocytes, and microglia, we demonstrated that CD40L and IFN-{gamma} trigger microglia-dependent neuronal dysregulation and death. Anti-CD40L treatment substantially attenuated neuronal injury. Finally, transcriptomic enrichment analysis identified CD40L-mediated microglial gene expression signatures that correlated with CD40 expression in primary progressive MS brain tissue but not in the normal-appearing white matter of healthy controls. Collectively, these findings support a role for the microglial CD40/T cell CD40L axis in MS pathology and suggest CD40L blockade as a potential therapeutic strategy warranting further investigation.

bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-03 · Zhou, J., Liao, H., Liang, W. … Liu, Y., Zhou, Z., Wu, S.（共 9 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer、neurodegeneration』(命中abstract)
Extensive synaptic loss and progressive neurodegeneration are defining pathological hallmarks of Alzheimer's disease (AD), primarily driven by soluble A{beta} oligomers and neurofibrillary tangles. The apolipoprotein E4 (APOE4) isoform further aggravates these pathological cascades, yet the precise molecular mechanisms underlying AD pathogenesis remain poorly elucidated. Store-operated calcium entry (SOCE) deficiency in neurons has been proven to exert a detrimental effect on dendritic spine integrity and function. Herein, we investigated whether SOCE dysregulation contributes to A{beta}O-induced synaptotoxicity and how APOE4 modulates this pathological process. In primary neuronal cultures, co-treatment with A{beta}O and APOE4 markedly reduced synapse density and impaired dendritic arbor complexity, compared with A{beta}O treatments combined with APOE3 or APOE2 isoforms. Consistently, neurons exposed to A{beta}O plus APOE4 exhibited significantly lower SOCE amplitudes than those treated with A{beta}O and APOE3. In line with SOCE suppression, the A{beta}O/APOE4 combination specifically downregulated stromal interaction molecule 1 (STIM1) protein expression, with no observable change in STIM2 levels. Mechanistically, A{beta}O/APOE4-mediated STIM1 reduction was dependent on p62-selective autophagic degradation. Notably, exogenous STIM1 overexpression effectively rescued the deficits in dendritic arbor complexity. Furthermore, STIM1 downregulation induced by A{beta}O and APOE4 initiated the autophagic degradation of glutathione peroxidase 4 (Gpx4). This autophagy activation was attributed to decreased Bcl2 expression and occurs independently of STING accumulation. Subsequent Gpx4 insufficiency further triggered excessive accumulation of reactive oxygen species and disrupted mitochondrial cristae morphology in neurons. Collectively, our findings uncover a previously unrecognized mechanism whereby APOE4 exacerbates A{beta}O-induced synaptotoxicity. Specifically, the combination facilitates p62-mediated selective autophagic degradation of STIM1, which suppresses Bcl2 expression and ultimately promotes robust Gpx4 autophagic degradation, driving neuronal synaptic damage.

Background Heart disease is a crucial risk factor for brain degeneration, but the specific mechanisms of this association are still under investigation. Notably, the development of white-matter lesions, a hallmark of cerebral small-vessel disease with connections to progressive neurodegeneration and cognitive decline, has been linked to cardiovascular disease. As such, measures of cardiovascular function such as heart-rate variability may represent accessible indicators of white-matter lesion burden. Methods This study used data from healthy adults drawn from the Leipzig Study for Mind-Body-Emotion Interactions (LEMON) dataset to examine heart function through measures of heart-rate variability, as well as their relationship to cerebral small-vessel disease as quantified through white-matter lesion volumes, using FreeSurfer SAMSEG. The unique association between reduced low-frequency band heart-rate variability and age-related lesion burden may help to establish the link between autonomic control of cardiac function and brain health in aging.

Introduction. Given interest in vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) as a potential therapy for Alzheimer's disease, due to its hypothesized influence on the noradrenergic (NA) system, there is a need to understand the pronounced individual differences in responsiveness to VNS in aging. We tested whether a genetic polymorphism related to NA signaling, the {Delta}301-303 deletion in the ADRA2B receptor, modulates transcutaneous auricular (taVNS) effects on task-related pupillary responses or pupil-linked cortical activity in noradrenergic projection territory. Methods. Forty-one adults (61% deletion carriers; ages 19-79) performed a visual oddball task during a sham-controlled single-blind crossover taVNS trial. We introduce the New Therapeutics in Alzheimer's Disease (NTAD) dataset, a multi-modal and longitudinal imaging dataset from 70 biomarker-positive people with early dementia or Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) due to Alzheimer's Disease (AD), assessed over 2.5 years, and 35 healthy controls assessed at baseline. Caudate nucleus aging and its association with brain disorders🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-03 · Guo, S., Yang, X., Dong, Y. … Galvan, A., Smith, Y., He, J.（共 9 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer』(命中abstract)
The caudate nucleus supports cognition and goal-directed behavior, undergoes age-associated changes, and is implicated in substance use, psychiatric, and neurodegenerative disorders. However, how aging alters its diverse cell types and how such changes relate to diseases remains poorly understood. To address these questions, we assembled single nucleus transcriptomic atlases of the caudate tissue, comprising 963,842 nuclei from 178 human donors, including controls and individuals with alcohol use disorder (AUD) or opioid use disorder (OUD), and 83,699 nuclei from six healthy rhesus macaques. We also curated 3,139 bulk RNA-seq profiles from the caudate and other brain regions spanning controls and multiple diagnostic groups, as well as paired RNA and chromatin accessibility profiles from 257,524 caudate nuclei across 161 control and AUD donors. These datasets enabled comparisons across age, species, brain regions, and diagnostic groups. We found shared glial aging features in humans and macaques, whereas neuronal changes were more divergent. Inferred neuron-to-neuron communication declined with age, with greater declines in AUD. Both transcriptional and chromatin accessibility profiles showed nonlinear age-associated patterns, with pronounced changes in later adulthood. In donors with AUD, age-associated changes appeared earlier, with altered trajectories in lipid metabolism, mitochondrial function, and neuronal signaling. Across comparisons involving AUD, OUD, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, and Alzheimer disease, mitochondrial function and fatty acid metabolism emerged as consensus features linking caudate aging to diseases, although the direction and extent of overlap varied by disorder and brain region. Together, these findings define cellular and regulatory features of primate caudate aging and reveal shared and context-dependent relationships between aging and brain disorders.

Topographic reference modelling of white matter hyperintensities across the adult lifespan🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-11-03 · Alshikho, M. J., Pyne, J. D., Edwards, N. C. … HABS-HD Study Team,, Alzheimers Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI),, Alzheimers Disease Sequencing Project（共 37 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer、neurodegeneration』(命中abstract)
White matter hyperintensities (WMH) are among the most common MRI findings in older adults. They are widely regarded as markers of brain aging and cerebral small-vessel disease, and associated with risk for cognitive decline, Alzheimers disease and related dementias (ADRD), and stroke. White matter hyperintensities (WMH) are typically interpreted by total lesion burden, but lesions with similar volumes can occupy different anatomical locations, potentially reflecting distinct pathological processes. Here we present voxelwise topographic reference modelling (vTRM), a framework for characterizing individual WMH masks against age- and sex-stratified frequency maps derived from cognitively unimpaired individuals. Using 42,469 FLAIR-derived lesion masks from twelve adult cohorts, we mapped population-level WMH spatial vulnerability, characterized age-related changes in its distribution across periventricular, lobar and arterial-territory compartments, and constructed paired percentile charts for lesion burden and spatial typicality. A quadrant-based classification defined by these two percentile dimensions revealed graded relationships with ADRD pathophysiology, neurodegeneration, and cognitive impairment, while identifying WMH patterns differentially associated with ageing-related, vascular and neurodegenerative processes among individuals with similar lesion volumes. These reference charts establish a benchmark for normative WMH ageing, enabling quantification of individual deviations from typical age-, sex- and burden-related patterns and identification of WMH phenotypes associated with vascular, neurodegenerative or mixed etiologies. The framework, together with a deployable 3D U-Net model trained on these data for automated WMH segmentation, is available through the Brain Lesion Analytics platform (https://brainlesionanalytics.org/).

关键词『Alzheimer、amyloid』(命中title)
Background Apolipoprotein E (apoE) plays numerous roles in the pathogenic pathway to Alzheimer's disease (AD). We previously performed a high-throughput screen and identified the antidepressant imipramine as an inhibitor of the interaction between apoE and amyloid-{beta} (A{beta}). We also found that imipramine use to treat depression in AD patients was associated with better Mini-Mental State Exam (MMSE) scores. Here we studied the mechanisms by which imipramine inhibits the AD pathogenic pathway and investigated its effect on mortality in AD patients. Methods Nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy was used to characterize the interaction between imipramine and A{beta} monomers. Human induced pluripotent stem cell (hiPSC)-derived AD cerebral organoids (COs) and transgenic AD rats treated with imipramine underwent neuropathological evaluation, while rat learning and memory were assessed in the Morris Water Maze. Brain mitovesicles undergo stage-specific remodelling during the progression of amyloid-β pathology🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增

Mitochondrial dysfunction is an early feature of Alzheimer's disease that shows nonlinear changes as the disease progresses. However, how these changes are reflected in the mitochondrial material released by brain cells remains poorly understood. Mitovesicles are a recently described extracellular vesicle subpopulation of mitochondrial origin that provide a unique insight into mitochondrial alterations in the brain and could potentially show how nonlinear changes evolve across disease stages of amyloid-beta pathology.
