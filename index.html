Cortical projection neurons exhibit early maturation defects and selective degeneration in a mouse model of spinal muscular atrophy🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-11 · Schellino, R., La Rocca, F., Iezzi, G., Caretto, A., Boido, M., Vercelli, A.
关键词『neurodegeneration』(命中abstract)
Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) is usually considered a lower motor neuron disease; however, growing evidence from both experimental models and clinical studies indicates that SMN deficiency affects the whole CNS, beyond the spinal and brainstem motor system. Here, we investigated the temporal progression and selective vulnerability of cortical projection neurons in the SMN{Delta}7 mouse model at early (P5) and late (P11) symptomatic stages. Combining subtype-specific immunohistochemistry, DiI tracing associated with morphometric reconstruction, and dendritic spine analysis, we identified stage- and subtype-dependent cortical alterations. At P11, corticospinal and infragranular callosal projection neurons were selectively reduced in number and showed marked dendritic remodeling and altered excitatory synaptic organization, whereas corticothalamic neurons and supragranular SATB2-positive populations were comparatively preserved. Notably, cortical abnormalities were already detectable at P5, before overt neuronal loss: indeed, corticospinal neurons exhibited reduced soma size and dendritic complexity, while dendritic spine density remained preserved, but spine morphology was shifted toward immature phenotypes. These early maturation defects persisted during disease progression and preceded the reduction in spine density observed at P11. Together, our findings identify cortical projection neurons as selectively vulnerable to constitutive SMN deficiency and suggest that impaired neuronal and synaptic maturation represents an early feature of cortical pathology preceding subtype-specific neurodegeneration. These results expand the pathophysiological spectrum of SMA beyond lower motor neurons and highlight early brain alterations as a potential component of disease progression.

A large, comprehensive labeled MRI dataset of the human lateral ventricle choroid plexus🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-11 · Huang, L.-Y., Zeng, V., Raymond, N. … Estrada, S., Reuter, M., Lizano, P.（共 22 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer』(命中abstract)
The choroid plexus (ChP) is a highly vascularized structure located in the brain ventricles that plays important roles in multiple central nervous system functions. ChP enlargement has been implicated in many neuropsychiatric conditions such as multiple sclerosis, depression, schizophrenia, and Alzheimer's disease. However, limitation in the accuracy of the current segmentation tools highlight the need for more reliable methods to quantify ChP volume in healthy and clinical human populations. The shape and size of the ChP vary drastically from person to person, and currently there are no large ChP datasets which allow comprehensive training of segmentation models. Here we introduce DataPlex, a collection of 700 structural MRI brain images where the lateral ventricle ChP has been manually labeled and checked by expert human raters. DataPlex is balanced and representative across a wide range of scanning parameters, demographics, and clinical profiles, and subject-level information will be available for each labeled image. DataPlex will be open source to support the creation of reliable segmentation tools for the human lateral ventricle ChP.

Continuous amyloid staging complements global amyloid burden in early Alzheimer's disease🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-11 · Liu, H., Tong, M., Fang, S. … Saykin, A. J., Yan, J., For the Alzheimers Disease Neuroimaging Initiative,（共 12 位作者）
关键词『Alzheimer、amyloid』(命中title)
Introduction: Early Alzheimer's disease (AD) staging remains challenging because clinical categories and global amyloid PET measures provide only coarse characterization of disease progression. We developed a continuous amyloid staging score from regional amyloid PET to complement global amyloid burden. Methods: Using longitudinal florbetapir PET from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI), we trained an end-to-end deep learning model to directly estimate a continuous amyloid staging score. We evaluated longitudinal consistency, its relationship with global amyloid burden, regional contributors to early-stage variation, and whether the staging score stratified subsequent memory decline among participants matched for baseline global amyloid burden. Cognitive analyses were replicated in A4+LEARN. Results: The staging score better preserved longitudinal visit order than existing amyloid staging approaches. During early disease stages, it captured regional amyloid variation despite relatively stable global amyloid burden. Among participants with similar baseline global amyloid burden, higher staging scores were associated with faster subsequent memory decline in both ADNI and A4+LEARN. Discussion: A continuous amyloid staging score complements global amyloid burden for early AD staging and cognitive risk stratification. Keywords: Alzheimer's disease; amyloid PET; disease staging; cognitive decline

Autonomic neurons are intrinsically hyperexcitable in a Friedreich ataxia iPSC model🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-11 · (Max), S. L., Lees, J., Zhang, H. … McCarthy, D., Napierala, J., Napierala, M.（共 14 位作者）
关键词『neurodegeneration』(命中abstract)
Friedreich ataxia (FRDA) is an inherited multisystem disorder caused by a GAA repeat expansion in the FXN gene and consequent frataxin deficiency, causing progressive neurodegeneration and heart disease. Clinical studies have documented autonomic dysfunction in FRDA, including elevated resting heart rate, raised plasma catecholamines and a high burden of urinary, bowel and sexual symptoms. Consistent with this, autopsy studies show degeneration of the cardiac conduction system and its associated ganglia in FRDA. Whether these features are intrinsic to FRDA autonomic neurons has been untestable without a human cellular model. Here, a human model of FRDA autonomic neurons was established by differentiating induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from individuals with FRDA and from their CRISPR/Cas9-corrected isogenic controls. FRDA autonomic neurons expressed canonical autonomic lineage markers and retained reduced FXN mRNA and frataxin protein levels. Electrophysiological analysis revealed pronounced hyperexcitability, with an approximately five-fold increase in spike number, shortened interspike intervals, and longer bursts. FRDA autonomic neurons also exhibited increased cell death, elevated mitochondrial reactive oxygen species, and a shift from networked to tubular mitochondrial morphology. Bulk RNA sequencing of all three isogenic pairs revealed loss of extracellular matrix programs and activation of stress response pathways. The top differentially expressed gene was MEG3, a maternally imprinted long noncoding RNA, which was consistently upregulated across all three isogenic pairs. MEG3 sits within the imprinted 14q32 DLK1-DIO3 locus, whose maternally expressed noncoding transcripts were co-ordinately de-repressed in FRDA autonomic neurons. Competitive gene-set testing of 43 imprinted genes across 20 domains showed this to be specific to 14q32: no other imprinted domain was co-ordinately altered. Together, these findings establish hyperexcitability of autonomic neurons as a cell autonomous consequence of frataxin deficiency, accompanied by mitochondrial dysfunction. This provides a cellular basis for the increased sympathetic tone observed in clinical studies.

ASC specks and α-synuclein amplify microglial NLRP3 inflammasome activation and co-localize with Lewy body pathology🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-10 · Thakur, K., Peladeau, C., Hewitt, M. … Tomlinson, J. T., Schlossmacher, M. G., Sandhu, J. K.（共 9 位作者）
关键词『Parkinson、amyloid』(命中abstract)
Neuroinflammation is a hallmark of several chronic neurodegenerative disorders, including Parkinson's disease (PD) and other synucleinopathies, and is closely associated with microglial activation. The inflammasome adaptor protein apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a caspase activation and recruitment domain (ASC) undergoes oligomerization upon inflammasome activation, resulting in the formation of ASC specks. These structures are recognized by microglia as damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), thereby promoting inflammatory signaling. Recent studies have demonstrated that extracellular ASC specks associate with amyloid-{beta} (A{beta}), and that uptake of ASC-A{beta} composites by microglia amplifies pro-inflammatory responses. Although ASC specks have also been shown to potentiate -synuclein (Syn) preformed fibril-induced pathology, their interaction with monomeric Syn and their effects on inflammasome activation remain poorly understood. Here, we co-stimulated murine SIM-A9 microglia with ASC-Syn assemblies and evaluated their effects on NLRP3 inflammasome activation. Electron microscopy revealed a close association between ASC fibrils and Syn monomers. Exposure of microglia to ASC-Syn enhanced NLRP3 inflammasome activation, as evidenced by increased ASC speck formation, caspase-1 activation, and secretion of IL-1{beta} and IL-18, when compared to ASC or Syn alone. In addition, immunohistochemical analyses of post mortem brain tissue from patients with dementia with Lewy bodies demonstrated ASC expression in regions exhibiting Syn pathology and showed ASC co-localization with Syn within Lewy bodies. Collectively, these findings identify ASC-Syn assemblies as potent amplifiers of NLRP3 inflammasome signaling and suggest a mechanism through which they may contribute to sustained neuroinflammation in synucleinopathies.

A Bayesian approach to volumetric analysis in rare neurodegenerative disorders🟢 提前在线NEW 本次新增
bioRxiv · Neuroscience · 在线发布 2026-10-10 · Ho, S. Y. S., Schmidt, T. V., Ghosh, B., Rowe, J., Rittman, T.
关键词『Parkinson』(命中abstract)
Identifying regional brain atrophy is critical for understanding neurodegenerative disorders. Traditionally, group comparisons rely on frequentist statistics, utilising $p$-values and point estimates that offer limited characterization of uncertainty. Bayesian methods provide an alternative by estimating effect sizes and credible intervals while incorporating prior knowledge and uncertainty. This study evaluated the feasibility of Bayesian hierarchical modelling to assess regional brain atrophy in Progressive Supranuclear Palsy (PSP) and Corticobasal Syndrome (CBS) compared to healthy controls. Participants meeting diagnostic criteria for CBS or PSP, together with healthy controls, were recruited from the Cambridge Centre for Parkinson-plus. Grey matter volume, cortical thickness, and subcortical volumes were derived using FreeSurfer for 210 cortical and 36 subcortical regions defined by the Human Brainnetome Atlas. Participants with PSP were classified into Richardson syndrome (PSP-RS), corticobasal syndrome variant (PSP-CBS), frontal variant (PSP-F), and other presentations (PSP-Other). Bayesian hierarchical ANOVA models with appropriate priors were used to estimate group differences. Regions of practical equivalence (ROPE) were calculated to assess evidence of meaningful group effects. A total of 428 people were included (113 PSP-RS, 30 PSP-CBS, 23 PSP-Other, 19 PSP-F, 81 CBS, and 162 healthy controls). For grey matter volume, there was evidence of whole brain volume loss compared to controls for all disease groups except PSP-Other, which showed evidence of no difference. For cortical thickness, there was evidence of global cortical thinning in CBS compared to controls, but for no other group. For subcortical volume all groups showed evidence of volume loss compared to controls. Patterns of regional atrophy were identified in keeping with previous literature, with the largest effect observed in the left pre-motor thalamus (30\% reduction in CBS). There was greater loss of subcortical volume in PSP-RS compared to other PSP subgroups. Bayesian hierarchical modelling provides a feasible framework for quantifying regional brain atrophy, with interpretable effect size estimates and uncertainty measures. This approach complements conventional statistical methods, particularly in the assessment of rare neurodegenerative and neuropsychiatric disorders.
